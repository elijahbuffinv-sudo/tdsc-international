      Republic of Austria

      Investigations Touching Austria

      In Austria, investigative work runs under the GDPR and the Datenschutzgesetz, works council co-determination over employee monitoring, and a regulated detective trade with an examination. Since 2022, access to the beneficial ownership register has also been restricted. TDSC scopes these matters from Nashville and brings in licensed Austrian detectives and counsel as needed.

      Overview

      Austria at a Glance

      Austria is a federal republic and EU member state with a civil law tradition. It regulates investigative activity more closely than many of its neighbours. Detective work (Berufsdetektive) is a regulated trade under the Gewerbeordnung. Practitioners must pass a competence examination and a reliability check. Personal data is governed by the GDPR together with the Austrian Datenschutzgesetz, enforced by the Datenschutzbehörde. Employee monitoring brings in the Labour Constitution Act, under which certain control measures need works council consent. Corporate transparency also narrowed after the Court of Justice of the European Union ruled in November 2022 that general public access to beneficial ownership registers was incompatible with the EU Charter. As a result, ownership research in Austria takes more care than it did a few years ago.

      Organisations commission investigative work involving Austria for several reasons. Vienna has long served as a regional hub for Central and Eastern Europe. Many international groups manage subsidiaries in the Balkans, Ukraine and the wider region from Austrian holding and management companies. Banking, insurance, energy trading, engineering and tourism are significant sectors. Austrian private foundations (Privatstiftungen) and holding structures appear often in cross-border ownership chains. Typical engagements include due diligence on Austrian holding entities and their regional operations, asset tracing involving foundations and real property, and integrity reviews of counterparties linked to Eastern European markets. Internal investigations at Austrian subsidiaries must account for works council rights.

      Typical Matters

      Where Investigative Work Involving Austria Concentrates

      01

      Regional holding company due diligence

      Many groups run Central and Eastern European operations through Vienna-based holding companies. Due diligence links Firmenbuch filings, filed accounts and litigation records with enquiries into subsidiaries across the Balkans and further east. The higher-risk exposures usually sit in those subsidiaries.

      02

      Private foundation and ownership tracing

      Privatstiftungen and layered GmbH structures can separate legal ownership from control. With public access to the beneficial ownership register closed since 2022, tracing relies on register extracts, filed documents, court records and, where counsel can show a legitimate interest, a properly made register request.

      03

      Works council compliant internal investigations

      Investigations into fraud or misconduct at Austrian subsidiaries need to account for works council consent requirements on control measures and for GDPR obligations. TDSC helps foreign parent companies plan document review and interviews so that the evidence stays usable in Austrian labour court proceedings.

      04

      Energy and trading counterparty reviews

      Vienna's role in gas and commodity trading, and in banking ties to Eastern markets, draws counterparties with complex ownership and political exposure. Engagements screen trading partners, intermediaries and their principals for integrity, sanctions-adjacent ownership and reputational risk before contracts are signed.

      05

      Asset tracing for enforcement

      Creditors enforcing judgments or Vienna-seated arbitral awards need to locate company holdings, real estate and foundation interests in Austria. TDSC works with Austrian counsel to map what lawful public sources can show and where court assistance is required.

      Law & Regulation

      What applies before any work touching Austria begins

      Legal system

      Austria has a civil law system built on codified statutes. Its core private law codes are the General Civil Code (ABGB) and the Commercial Code (UGB). Commercial disputes go to the ordinary courts. In Vienna, a dedicated Commercial Court (Handelsgericht Wien) hears commercial matters at first instance. Appeals run to the higher regional courts and then to the Supreme Court (Oberster Gerichtshof). Vienna is an established seat for international arbitration, and the Vienna International Arbitral Centre (VIAC) administers cases under its Vienna Rules.

      Data protection

      General Data Protection Regulation (EU) 2016/679 and Austrian Data Protection Act (Datenschutzgesetz, DSG), as adapted in 2018 and last amended 2024 · Regulator: Datenschutzbehörde (Austrian Data Protection Authority)

      Investigative processing of personal data needs a GDPR lawful basis, usually legitimate interests or the establishment and defence of legal claims. It must be proportionate and documented. Data on criminal offences is restricted under Article 10 GDPR, and extensive processing of such data may require a data protection impact assessment. Employee data is not systematically covered by the DSG. Instead, the Labour Constitution Act (ArbVG), particularly sections 96 and 96a, requires works council consent for certain control systems. Austrian commentary treats a missing works agreement as a data protection violation in its own right. Transfers outside the EEA require an adequacy decision, Standard Contractual Clauses or another GDPR mechanism. US recipients may rely on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework where they are certified.

      Private investigators

      Detective work is a regulated trade (reglementiertes Gewerbe) under the Gewerbeordnung, within the security trades. Beyond the general conditions for trade, applicants must provide proof of competence (Befähigungsnachweis). This normally means passing an examination before a commission after several years of police, criminal investigation or detective experience. The exam covers criminology, criminal law, constitutional and administrative law, and criminal and civil procedure. Authorities also carry out a reliability check that goes beyond a criminal record extract. Detectives hold no state powers, and their surveillance and recording are subject to the GDPR, the DSG and criminal law. We have not confirmed the scope of permitted activities in the trade regulations from a primary source.

      Corporate & public records

      The Firmenbuch (commercial register) is maintained by the judiciary and accessed through the Justiz online portal. Basic data such as name, register number, legal form, seat and status can be searched without charge. Full current and historical extracts, which show officers and GmbH shareholders, and filed annual accounts are available for a fee. Access requires an Austrian ID or EU Login, which can limit direct access from outside the EU. The beneficial ownership register under the WiEReG is kept by the Federal Ministry of Finance. Since the CJEU judgment of 22 November 2022, it has been open only to obliged entities and to persons who show a legitimate interest.

      Anti-corruption & AML

      Bribery of public officials, including foreign officials, and private sector bribery are offences under the Criminal Code (StGB). Companies can be held liable under the Corporate Liability Act (Verbandsverantwortlichkeitsgesetz, VbVG). A 2023 amendment, approved in July 2023 and in force from September 2023, criminalised mandate buying, reached conduct before a candidate takes office, and raised penalties. The Public Prosecutor's Office for White-Collar Crime and Corruption (WKStA) prosecutes major cases. It works with the Federal Bureau of Anti-Corruption (BAK), established within the Interior Ministry in 2010.

      Engagement Standard

      How TDSC Scopes Matters Touching Austria

      TDSC first checks whether a matter involves Austrian employees, Austrian data subjects or evidence intended for Austrian courts. Each of these brings different constraints. Desk research draws on Firmenbuch extracts and filed accounts, court and insolvency publications, media archives and reputable commercial databases. Beneficial ownership information is sought only through channels open to an obliged entity or to a party with a demonstrable legitimate interest, normally through Austrian counsel. Any work on the ground, such as observation or local enquiries, is coordinated with licensed Berufsdetektive. Workplace matters are planned with counsel so that works council rights are respected. Personal data is minimised and documented against a GDPR lawful basis, and transfers to Nashville rely on a recognised transfer mechanism.

      TDSC has no office in Austria. Engagements are directed from Nashville, Tennessee; where work on the ground is needed, it is performed by appropriately licensed local professionals under a written scope. TDSC does not provide legal advice.

      Questions

      Austria: Frequently Asked Questions

      Are private detectives licensed in Austria?

      Yes. Berufsdetektive work in a regulated trade under the Gewerbeordnung. To enter it, a person must pass a competence examination before a commission, normally after relevant police or investigative experience, and must pass a reliability check. TDSC does not hold an Austrian trade licence. Where work in Austria is needed, it coordinates with detectives who are authorised there.

      Can we still search the Austrian beneficial ownership register?

      Not freely. After the CJEU ruled on 22 November 2022 that unrestricted public access breached fundamental rights, Austria withdrew public access. Obliged entities and persons who can show a legitimate interest may still obtain information. For most investigative purposes, ownership is reconstructed from the Firmenbuch, filed accounts and other lawful records.

      Do we need the works council's agreement before investigating staff?

      Possibly. Under the Labour Constitution Act, certain control measures that affect employees require works council consent. Austrian commentary treats a missing works agreement as making related processing unlawful under the GDPR. Before any monitoring, device review or systematic data analysis, the measure should be assessed with Austrian employment counsel.

      Why can't our team pull full Firmenbuch extracts from the United States?

      The justice portal requires an Austrian ID or EU Login to access full extracts and documents beyond basic data, so non-EU users are often blocked. TDSC obtains extracts through appropriate channels and keeps a record of the source and retrieval date so that the document's provenance is clear.

      What changed in Austrian anti-corruption law in 2023?

      An amendment approved in July 2023, in force from September 2023, made buying a mandate a criminal offence, extended liability to corrupt conduct before a person takes office, and raised penalties for corruption offences. Corporate liability continues under the VbVG. Integrity reviews of politically connected counterparties should take these changes into account.
