      Romania

      Romania: Investigative Consulting

      Investigative consulting for matters touching Romania involves EU data protection rules, a national trade registry, slow but document-rich court proceedings and an active anti-corruption prosecution service. Engagements typically combine Romanian-language records research, procurement and integrity due diligence and coordination with detectives licensed under Romanian law.

      Overview

      Romania at a Glance

      Romania is an EU member state with a civil law system, a codified Civil Code and a judiciary that is constitutionally independent but often slow, with appeals that can be prolonged and enforcement that can be difficult. There are no specialised commercial courts, so business disputes are heard by the general civil courts. Company information is held by the National Trade Register Office, and the General Data Protection Regulation applies alongside national implementing law and strict rules on employee monitoring. Investigators working on Romanian matters must read Romanian-language filings and judgments, understand how public procurement and integrity rules operate, and plan around the GDPR from the first step rather than as an afterthought.

      Organisations commission work involving Romania because of its role as a manufacturing, IT services and energy hub within the EU, with substantial foreign investment in automotive, telecommunications, defence, healthcare, banking and insurance. EU funds and public infrastructure spending create large procurement programmes in which contractors, subcontractors and consultants come under scrutiny. Typical exposures include conflicts of interest in public tenders, undisclosed related parties, misappropriation by local management, fraud involving EU or state funds, and counterparties with past or pending investigations. Investors and lenders also commission background research on Romanian shareholders and managers before acquisitions, joint ventures and financing.

      Background research on bidders, subcontractors and consultants in infrastructure, energy and EU-funded programmes, examining shareholder links, conflicts of interest, integrity findings, DNA cases and adverse media, to support contracting authorities' counterparts, lenders and compliance teams assessing exposure.

      Internal investigations at Romanian subsidiaries into suspected kickbacks, diversion of sales or related-party supply arrangements, planned around Law No. 190/2018 monitoring conditions, employee consultation requirements and the Romanian Labour Code before devices or communications are reviewed.

      Pre-transaction reviews of Romanian companies and principals in IT, manufacturing, agriculture, healthcare and real estate, combining trade registry history, financial filings, litigation, insolvency records and Romanian-language media to identify undisclosed interests and reputational issues.

      Law & Regulation

      What applies before any work touching Romania begins

      Legal system

      Romania has a civil law system. Private law is codified in the Civil Code (Law No. 287/2009, in force since 2011) and procedure in the Code of Civil Procedure. Commercial disputes are heard by the ordinary civil courts, as there are no specialised commercial courts: courts of first instance, tribunals, courts of appeal and the High Court of Cassation and Justice. Arbitration is available through the Court of International Commercial Arbitration attached to the Chamber of Commerce and Industry of Romania and the Bucharest International Arbitration Court, founded in 2016 by AmCham Romania.

      Data protection

      General Data Protection Regulation (EU) 2016/679 and Law No. 190/2018 on measures implementing the GDPR · Regulator: National Supervisory Authority for Personal Data Processing (ANSPDCP)

      The GDPR applies directly in Romania and Law No. 190/2018 adds national rules. Employee monitoring through electronic communications or video surveillance, based on legitimate interest, requires a thorough justification that outweighs employees' rights, complete prior notice to employees, prior consultation with the trade union or employee representatives, evidence that less intrusive methods were ineffective, and retention generally limited to 30 days. Processing of the national identification number (CNP) on a legitimate-interest basis requires specific safeguards, including appointment of a data protection officer. Transfers outside the EEA follow the GDPR transfer mechanisms.

      Private investigators

      Private detective work in Romania is regulated by Law No. 329/2003 on the exercise of the profession of private detective, as amended and republished. Individuals must hold a professional attestation and companies conducting detective activity require a licence, both administered through the Romanian Police (General Inspectorate of Romanian Police). Detectives may not use methods reserved to state authorities, such as interception of communications, and their activity is subject to the GDPR and the Criminal Code's protections for private life. Field inquiries in Romania therefore require a licensed Romanian detective working within those limits.

      Corporate & public records

      Companies are registered with the National Trade Register Office (Oficiul National al Registrului Comertului, ONRC), which provides online services in Romanian, including extracts showing registered office, share capital, shareholders of limited liability companies, directors and filing history. Annual financial statements filed with the tax authority are publicly searchable in summary form. A register of beneficial owners is kept by the ONRC under the anti-money laundering law; following the 2022 Court of Justice of the EU ruling on public access to such registers, access is now generally limited to authorities, obliged entities and persons demonstrating a legitimate interest. Court case information is published through the courts' portal, and land book extracts are issued by the cadastre agency.

      Anti-corruption & AML

      Corruption offences are set out in the Criminal Code and Law No. 78/2000 on preventing, detecting and sanctioning acts of corruption. The National Anticorruption Directorate (DNA) prosecutes high-level corruption, the National Integrity Agency (ANI) verifies assets, incompatibilities and conflicts of interest of officials, and the National Agency for Managing Seized Assets (ANABI) supports asset recovery. Anti-money laundering obligations are set by Law No. 129/2019, with the National Office for the Prevention and Control of Money Laundering (ONPCSB) as the financial intelligence unit. Romania joined the OECD Anti-Bribery Convention in September 2023.

      Engagement Standard

      How TDSC Scopes Matters Touching Romania

      TDSC scopes Romanian engagements from Nashville with a GDPR assessment at the outset: purpose, lawful basis, minimisation and the transfer mechanism for any personal data leaving the EEA. Desk research draws on trade registry extracts, published financial statements, court portal records, insolvency notices, procurement data and Romanian-language media, reviewed in Romanian. Where interviews, site visits or observation are required, TDSC coordinates detectives holding attestations and licences under Law No. 329/2003 and works with Romanian counsel on employee monitoring conditions and evidence use. Beneficial ownership information is sought only through channels for which the client or counsel has a legitimate interest. TDSC does not hold a Romanian licence and does not provide legal advice.

      TDSC has no office in Romania. Engagements are directed from Nashville, Tennessee; where work on the ground is needed, it is performed by appropriately licensed local professionals under a written scope. TDSC does not provide legal advice.

      Questions

      Romania: Frequently Asked Questions

      Are private detectives licensed in Romania?

      Yes. Law No. 329/2003 regulates the profession. Individuals need a professional attestation and detective companies need a licence, administered through the Romanian Police. Detectives cannot intercept communications or use methods reserved to the state. TDSC coordinates licensed Romanian detectives for any fieldwork and performs records research from Nashville.

      Can we review a Romanian employee's email in an internal investigation?

      Possibly, but only within strict conditions. Law No. 190/2018 requires justified legitimate interest, complete prior notice, prior consultation with employee representatives, proof that less intrusive methods failed and limited retention. Romanian employment counsel should approve the review plan before any data is accessed.

      Can we see who owns a Romanian company?

      Shareholders of limited liability companies appear in trade registry extracts. The beneficial ownership register is not open to the general public following the 2022 EU court ruling; access generally requires a legitimate interest. Where that route is not available, ownership is reconstructed from registry history, filings, litigation and media.

      How useful are Romanian court records for due diligence?

      Case lists and hearing information are published through the courts' online portal, which allows searches by party name, and many judgments are available. Records show parties and procedural stages but not always the full file. Because proceedings and appeals can run for years, a pending case often matters as much as a final judgment.

      Does TDSC research anti-corruption exposure of Romanian counterparties?

      Yes. We review public information on DNA prosecutions, National Integrity Agency findings, procurement records and media coverage involving a counterparty and its principals. We report what is documented and its procedural status, distinguishing allegations, indictments and convictions, and we do not access restricted investigative files.
