      Overview

      Ireland at a Glance

      Ireland is a common law jurisdiction and EU member state whose legal environment is familiar to US and UK clients but shaped by EU regulation. Personal data is governed by the GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018, and the Data Protection Commission acts as lead supervisory authority for many multinationals with European headquarters in Dublin. Private investigators are among the security services regulated by the Private Security Authority, established in 2004. Company information is filed with the Companies Registration Office, which since 2019 has also maintained the Register of Beneficial Ownership, with access divided into tiers. The combination of common law procedure, detailed company filings and active data protection supervision means investigative work is feasible but must be carefully documented.

      Organisations commission investigative work involving Ireland because of its role as the European base for many technology, pharmaceutical, medical device and financial services groups, and as a centre for aircraft leasing and funds administration. Irish holding and operating companies frequently sit between US parents and their European, Middle Eastern and African businesses. Typical engagements include due diligence on Irish entities and their directors, asset tracing in support of litigation or enforcement, integrity reviews of counterparties in leasing and funds structures, and support for internal investigations at Irish subsidiaries, where employee data, protected disclosures and regulatory reporting obligations need to be handled together.

      Law & Regulation

      What applies before any work touching Ireland begins

      Legal system

      Ireland has a common law system under a written Constitution, with statute law including the Companies Act 2014 governing company matters. Civil disputes are heard by the District Court, Circuit Court and High Court according to value and subject, with appeals to the Court of Appeal and the Supreme Court. High-value commercial disputes can be admitted to the Commercial Court, a specialist list of the High Court that applies active case management. Dublin is also used as a seat for international arbitration under the Arbitration Act 2010, which applies the UNCITRAL Model Law.

      Data protection

      General Data Protection Regulation (EU) 2016/679 and Data Protection Act 2018 · Regulator: Data Protection Commission (An Coimisiún um Chosaint Sonraí)

      Investigative processing needs a GDPR lawful basis, usually legitimate interests or the establishment and defence of legal claims, and must be proportionate and documented. The 2018 Act extends criminal offence data to cover alleged offences and related proceedings, and section 55 allows such processing only in limited circumstances, such as legal claims or explicit consent, with suitable safeguards. It is an offence for an employer to compel an individual to make a subject access request, for example to obtain records for vetting. Transfers outside the EEA require an adequacy decision, Standard Contractual Clauses or another GDPR mechanism; certified US recipients may rely on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework.

      Private investigators

      Private investigators are one of the security sectors regulated by the Private Security Authority (PSA), the statutory body established on 29 October 2004 under the Private Security Services Act 2004. The PSA licenses providers of regulated security services and sets the standards they must meet. Licensed investigators hold no state powers. Surveillance, recording and the handling of personal data remain subject to the GDPR, the Data Protection Act 2018, constitutional privacy protections and criminal law. We have not confirmed from a primary source the date on which licensing became mandatory for private investigators or the current licence categories, so these are checked before engagement.

      Corporate & public records

      The Companies Registration Office (CRO), an office of the Department of Enterprise, Tourism and Employment, incorporates companies, registers filings and makes company information available to the public, including through its online services. Filed documents typically include constitutions, director and secretary details, annual returns and, for many companies, financial statements. Since 2019 the CRO has maintained the Register of Beneficial Ownership (RBO), recording individuals who own or control more than 25 per cent. Access is tiered: competent authorities have full access, designated persons have restricted access, and members of the public see only the number of beneficial owners filed.

      Anti-corruption & AML

      The Criminal Justice (Corruption Offences) Act 2018 consolidated Irish bribery law, covering public and private sector corruption, foreign officials and corporate liability. The Criminal Justice (Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing) Act 2010, as amended, sets anti-money laundering duties for designated persons. The Garda National Economic Crime Bureau investigates serious fraud and corruption, the Corporate Enforcement Authority enforces company law, and the Central Bank of Ireland supervises financial institutions.

      Typical Matters

      Where Investigative Work Involving Ireland Concentrates

      01

      Due diligence on Irish entities

      Irish holding and operating companies often sit between US parents and their international businesses. Due diligence combines CRO filings, director histories, published judgments and media coverage to establish who runs an Irish counterparty, how it is financed and whether it has a record of regulatory or insolvency issues.

      02

      Aircraft leasing and funds counterparties

      Ireland is a centre for aircraft leasing and investment funds, where special purpose vehicles and layered structures are common. Engagements review lessees, investors, intermediaries and their principals for integrity concerns, sanctions-adjacent ownership and links to politically exposed persons.

      03

      Internal investigations at Irish subsidiaries

      Inquiries into fraud or misconduct must account for employee data rights, the protected disclosures regime for whistleblowers and fair procedures in disciplinary processes. TDSC helps foreign parent companies plan document review and interviews with Irish counsel so that findings stand up before Irish tribunals and courts.

      04

      Asset tracing for litigation and enforcement

      Creditors and litigants often need to identify Irish companies, property and other assets connected to a debtor. TDSC maps what CRO filings, court records and other lawful sources show and works with Irish counsel on disclosure orders and freezing relief available through the courts.

      05

      Director and executive background reviews

      Before appointments, investments or partnerships, clients often need a verified picture of an individual's directorships, business record and public profile in Ireland. Reviews draw on CRO records, published judgments and reputable media, within the limits set by the GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018.

      Engagement Standard

      How TDSC Scopes Matters Touching Ireland

      TDSC begins by establishing whether a matter involves Irish employees, Irish data subjects, regulated financial entities or evidence intended for Irish courts, because each carries different obligations. Desk research uses CRO filings, published judgments, insolvency and regulatory notices, media archives and reputable commercial databases. Beneficial ownership information is sought only through access available to a designated person or through Irish counsel. Any activity in Ireland that falls within regulated private investigation, such as observation, process-related enquiries or witness tracing, is carried out by investigators licensed by the PSA. Personal data is minimised and documented against a GDPR lawful basis, criminal offence data is handled under section 55 of the 2018 Act, and transfers to Nashville rely on a recognised transfer mechanism.

      TDSC has no office in Ireland. Engagements are directed from Nashville, Tennessee; where work on the ground is needed, it is performed by appropriately licensed local professionals under a written scope. TDSC does not provide legal advice.

      Questions

      Ireland: Frequently Asked Questions

      Are private investigators regulated in Ireland?

      Yes. Private investigators are among the security sectors regulated by the Private Security Authority, established in 2004 under the Private Security Services Act 2004. Providers of regulated services require a PSA licence and must meet its standards. TDSC does not hold a PSA licence and coordinates with licensed Irish investigators where work in Ireland requires one.

      Can the public see who owns an Irish company?

      Only to a limited extent. The Register of Beneficial Ownership operates in tiers: competent authorities have full access, designated persons such as banks and other regulated firms have restricted access, and members of the public can see only the number of beneficial owners filed. Ownership research therefore relies heavily on CRO filings and other lawful records.

      Can an employer ask a candidate to obtain their own records through a subject access request?

      No. The Data Protection Act 2018 makes it an offence for an employer or prospective employer to require an individual to make a subject access request, for example to obtain records for vetting. Background screening in Ireland must use lawful channels and an appropriate GDPR basis instead.

      Why does the Data Protection Commission matter beyond Ireland?

      Many multinationals have their main EU establishment in Ireland, which makes the Data Protection Commission their lead supervisory authority under the GDPR's one-stop-shop mechanism. Investigations involving these companies' European operations may therefore be shaped by Irish data protection practice even when the facts lie elsewhere in the EU.

      How are allegations of offences handled in an investigative report?

      Carefully. The Data Protection Act 2018 treats data on alleged offences and related proceedings as criminal offence data, which may be processed only in limited circumstances, such as for legal claims, and with suitable safeguards. Reports are scoped with Irish counsel so that such information is recorded only where it is lawful and necessary.
