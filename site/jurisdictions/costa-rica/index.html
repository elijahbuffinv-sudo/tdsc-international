      Overview

      Costa Rica at a Glance

      Costa Rica has a stable constitutional democracy, an export economy built around free trade zones, and a public administration with a long tradition of formal registration. The Registro Nacional holds companies, real property and movable assets, and much of it can be consulted online. Ownership transparency is split: shareholder and beneficial ownership data are reported to the Registro de Transparencia y Beneficiarios Finales, which is not public. Personal data processing is governed by Law 8968, and private investigation is a regulated activity under the private security statute, Law 8395 of 2003. Investigative work therefore turns on careful use of public sources and properly authorised local professionals.

      Organisations commission work involving Costa Rica for reasons tied to its economic profile. Medical device, electronics and life sciences manufacturers operate in free trade zones, and shared services and nearshore technology centres employ large workforces. Tourism and real estate along the Pacific coast attract foreign buyers and developers. Exposures include counterparty and partner vetting, misappropriation by local managers, disputed land and concession title, the use of sociedades anónimas to hold property, and asset location for judgment enforcement. Corporate anti-bribery liability introduced in 2019, and expanded since, has also increased demand for compliance-driven inquiries.

      Typical Matters

      Where Investigative Work Involving Costa Rica Concentrates

      01

      Free trade zone partner vetting

      Background and reputational due diligence on distributors, contract manufacturers, logistics providers and landlords serving operations in Costa Rica's free trade zones, focusing on registered representatives, litigation history, encumbrances on assets and adverse media in Spanish-language press.

      02

      Real estate and title verification

      Review of Registro Nacional property records, encumbrances, maritime-terrestrial zone concessions and the corporate vehicles holding coastal property, supporting buyers, lenders and developers in Guanacaste, Puntarenas and other areas where title, concession and zoning disputes recur.

      03

      Asset tracing for enforcement

      Identification of real property, registered vehicles and corporate interests held directly or through sociedades anónimas, to support recognition and enforcement of foreign judgments or arbitral awards, coordinated with Costa Rican counsel who can pursue precautionary measures.

      04

      Internal misconduct and fraud

      Fact-finding into procurement fraud, conflicts of interest and expense abuse at shared services centres and local subsidiaries, scoped around Law 8968, labour law constraints on employee monitoring and the Law 9699 compliance model that many groups now maintain.

      05

      Bribery and third-party risk

      Assessment of intermediaries, customs brokers and consultants dealing with public bodies, in light of corporate liability under Law 9699 and Law 10373, and of public procurement exposures with state institutions and autonomous entities.

      Law & Regulation

      What applies before any work touching Costa Rica begins

      Legal system

      Costa Rica has a civil law system based on codified law, including the Civil Code, Commercial Code and the Civil Procedure Code (Law 9342), which introduced oral, hearing-based civil proceedings. Commercial disputes are heard by civil courts of first instance and appellate tribunals, with cassation before the First Chamber of the Supreme Court; the Constitutional Chamber (Sala IV) hears amparo and constitutional claims. Arbitration is governed by Law 8937 of 2011, which a 2024 reform (Law 10535) made the single arbitration statute. Established centres include the Chamber of Commerce arbitration centre and CICA, attached to AmCham Costa Rica.

      Data protection

      Law No. 8968 on the Protection of Persons with respect to the Processing of their Personal Data (2011) and its Regulation (Executive Decree 37554-JP) · Regulator: Agencia de Protección de Datos de los Habitantes (PRODHAB)

      Law 8968 rests on informed consent, with exceptions, and gives individuals rights of access, rectification and deletion. Databases that are distributed, disclosed or commercialised must be registered with PRODHAB, while purely internal databases are outside registration. Transfers of personal data generally require the data subject's prior consent, although transfers to service providers, technology intermediaries and companies in the same economic group are not treated as transfers. For investigative work this means confirming a lawful basis for collection, avoiding data brokers whose databases are not properly registered, and documenting any transfer of findings abroad. A bill to align the law with the GDPR has been before the Legislative Assembly since 2021.

      Private investigators

      Private investigation is a regulated private security service. Law 8395 of 2003, the Law Regulating Private Security Services, lists private investigations among its service categories and provides that investigations to clarify specific facts may only be carried out by individuals or companies registered and authorised by the Dirección de Servicios de Seguridad Privados of the Ministry of Public Security. Individual investigators must meet education, training and psychological requirements and hold a credential renewed every two years. The law prohibits violating correspondence, intercepting communications, infringing privacy or a person's image, detaining or questioning people, and impersonating judicial or administrative authorities. Operating without authorisation is an offence.

      Corporate & public records

      The Registro Nacional maintains the companies registry (personas jurídicas), the real property registry and the movable property registry, and records such as legal representatives, registered capital, by-laws and property title and encumbrances are available through its online services. Shareholders are not generally listed in the public company record. Under Law 9416 of 2016, companies and trusts report shareholders and beneficial owners annually to the Registro de Transparencia y Beneficiarios Finales, administered on a Central Bank platform; the information is confidential and accessible only to the Ministry of Finance and the Costa Rican Drug Institute (ICD) under the law's conditions. Court files are generally accessible to parties through the Judiciary.

      Anti-corruption & AML

      The Law against Corruption and Illicit Enrichment in the Public Service (Law 8422, 2004) sets out public-sector corruption offences. Law 9699 of 2019 introduced liability of legal entities for domestic and transnational bribery and related offences, with an optional compliance model that can mitigate penalties; Law 10373 (2023) extended it in line with OECD recommendations. Money laundering is addressed by Law 7786, with the Financial Intelligence Unit of the ICD receiving suspicious transaction reports. Prosecution rests with the Public Prosecutor's Office (Ministerio Público).

      Engagement Standard

      How TDSC Scopes Matters Touching Costa Rica

      TDSC scopes Costa Rican matters from Nashville, beginning with a jurisdictional review of what can lawfully be gathered and by whom. Because Law 8395 reserves private investigation to persons authorised by the Dirección de Servicios de Seguridad Privados, any work performed in Costa Rica, such as field enquiries or site visits, is coordinated with appropriately authorised local professionals, and legal questions are referred to Costa Rican counsel. Desk research relies on Registro Nacional records, court information available to the relevant party, and Spanish-language open sources. TDSC does not seek the confidential beneficial ownership register, which is restricted to designated authorities. Personal data is collected only where a lawful basis under Law 8968 exists, minimised, and transferred to clients with documented safeguards.

      TDSC has no office in Costa Rica. Engagements are directed from Nashville, Tennessee; where work on the ground is needed, it is performed by appropriately licensed local professionals under a written scope. TDSC does not provide legal advice.

      Questions

      Costa Rica: Frequently Asked Questions

      Are private investigators licensed in Costa Rica?

      Yes. Law 8395 of 2003 treats private investigation as a private security service. Individuals and companies must be registered and authorised by the Dirección de Servicios de Seguridad Privados of the Ministry of Public Security, and individual investigators carry a credential that is renewed every two years. TDSC is not licensed in Costa Rica and coordinates authorised local professionals where local work is needed.

      Can we find out who owns a Costa Rican sociedad anónima?

      Partly. The Registro Nacional shows legal representatives and corporate details, but shareholders are generally not part of the public record. Beneficial ownership declared under Law 9416 is held in a confidential register that only the Ministry of Finance and the ICD may consult. Ownership is usually inferred from corporate documents, property records, litigation and other lawful sources.

      Does Law 8968 restrict background checks on individuals?

      It shapes them. Personal data must be processed lawfully, generally with consent subject to statutory exceptions, and databases that commercialise personal data must be registered with PRODHAB. Background enquiries are therefore designed around public records and lawful sources, with attention to the purpose of the enquiry and to how results are shared with the client outside Costa Rica.

      Can coastal property be checked before a purchase?

      Yes. Registro Nacional records show title, cadastral references and registered encumbrances, and concessions in the maritime-terrestrial zone are subject to their own rules administered by the municipalities. Due diligence typically combines registry review with corporate research on the holding company and litigation searches, with Costa Rican counsel and a notary handling the transaction itself.

      Is covert recording permitted in investigations?

      Law 8395 prohibits private security providers, including investigators, from intercepting communications, violating correspondence, or infringing privacy and a person's image. Covert recording of conversations or private spaces therefore carries significant legal risk. Any surveillance-type activity is reviewed with Costa Rican counsel before it is considered, and many matters are resolved without it.
