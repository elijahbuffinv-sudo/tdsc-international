      Overview

      North Macedonia at a Glance

      North Macedonia is a small, open economy and an EU candidate country since 2005, steadily aligning its legislation with EU standards. It has a civil law system, a one-stop Central Registry through which all legal entities are registered, and a Register of Ultimate Beneficial Owners launched in 2021. Its data protection law, in force since February 2020, closely follows the GDPR, and the regulator takes a conservative approach to transfers outside the EU, EEA and NATO countries. Investors and the US government have reported lengthy commercial litigation and concerns about political interference in the judiciary, which shapes how court records and enforcement prospects should be read.

      Organisations commission investigative work involving North Macedonia chiefly in connection with manufacturing investment, especially automotive and electronics suppliers operating in the Technological Industrial Development Zones, as well as energy, construction, public procurement and regional trade with the Western Balkans and the EU. Typical needs are background and ownership checks on local partners and suppliers, review of exposure to politically connected businesses, integrity due diligence ahead of acquisitions or tenders, and support to counsel in commercial disputes and judgment enforcement. Changes to the Criminal Code in 2023, which led to many corruption cases being dropped, mean that the absence of a conviction should not be read as the absence of concern.

      Jurisdictional Framework

      The rules that govern lawful inquiry in North Macedonia

      Legal system

      North Macedonia has a civil law system with increasing adversarial elements in procedure. Commercial disputes are heard by the ordinary courts, with appeals to the appellate courts and the Supreme Court; a Constitutional Court reviews constitutionality. Private enforcement agents carry out court judgments. Commercial disputes up to EUR 15,000 must first go through mediation. The Law on International Commercial Arbitration (2006) is based on the UNCITRAL Model Law, and a Permanent Court of Arbitration has operated within the Economic Chamber of North Macedonia since 1993.

      Data protection

      Law on Personal Data Protection (in force February 2020, as amended in 2021 and 2025) · Regulator: Personal Data Protection Agency

      The law is largely harmonised with the GDPR, so investigative work involving personal data needs a lawful basis, proportionate scope and transparency, including for employee and counterparty checks. Transfers to EU, EEA and NATO member countries require notification to the Agency at least 15 days in advance. Transfers elsewhere, including to the United States outside those categories, generally need appropriate safeguards and prior approval, and the Agency has adopted no adequacy decisions and has informally expected a transfer impact assessment since 2022. Misuse of personal data is also a criminal offence under the Criminal Code.

      Private investigators

      North Macedonia is understood to have specific legislation on detective activity with state authorisation, but we have not been able to open the current text or confirm the licensing authority and its procedures, so this point is confirmed with Macedonian counsel for each engagement. In any case, investigative work must respect the data protection law, criminal prohibitions on misuse of personal data and unauthorised recording, and the restriction of bank, telecoms and police records to lawful process. TDSC relies on appropriately licensed local professionals for any work requiring a local licence.

      Corporate & public records

      All legal entities register with the Central Registry of the Republic of North Macedonia through a one-stop-shop system, with online filing through authorised registration agents. Registry extracts show company details, founders, managers and registered capital, and annual accounts are filed with the Registry. North Macedonia also maintains a Register of Ultimate Beneficial Owners, launched in 2021 with a 25% threshold; the extent of public access should be confirmed at the time of a search. Real property records are held by the Agency for Real Estate Cadastre, and court records are obtained through the relevant court.

      Anti-corruption & AML

      Bribery, abuse of official position and related offences are criminalised under the Criminal Code, and officials and their close relatives must declare income and assets. The State Commission for Prevention of Corruption (DKSK) oversees prevention, conflicts of interest, asset declarations and lobbying, under the 2021-2025 national anti-corruption strategy. Prosecution sits with the public prosecution service. North Macedonia has ratified the UN Convention against Corruption. Criminal Code amendments in September 2023 led to many corruption cases being dropped.

      How TDSC Scopes Matters Touching North Macedonia

      TDSC scopes engagements touching North Macedonia from Nashville, beginning with a jurisdictional review of purpose, the lawful basis for any personal data processing and how results will be transferred to the client, given the Personal Data Protection Agency's approach to transfers outside the EU, EEA and NATO.       Questions

      North Macedonia: Frequently Asked Questions

      Can beneficial owners of a Macedonian company be identified?

      Often. North Macedonia has operated a Register of Ultimate Beneficial Owners since 2021, with a 25% threshold, alongside Central Registry records on founders and managers. Declarations are made by the companies themselves, so they should be cross-checked against shareholding history, related companies and media reporting, especially where foreign holding companies sit in the chain.

      Can investigative findings about Macedonian individuals be sent to the United States?

      Yes, but on a documented basis. The United States is a NATO member, so transfers can fall within the notification route, which requires notifying the Personal Data Protection Agency at least 15 days beforehand. Other destinations generally need safeguards and approval. TDSC keeps personal data to what the matter requires and confirms the transfer route with local counsel.

      Why might a corruption case against a counterparty have been dropped?

      Amendments to the Criminal Code in September 2023 reduced penalties and limitation periods for certain offences, and many pending cases were dismissed as a result. A discontinued case is therefore not the same as an acquittal on the merits. TDSC reports the procedural history and leaves the legal assessment to counsel.

      How reliable are court timelines for enforcing a claim in North Macedonia?

      Investors report that commercial litigation can be lengthy and costly, though judgments are enforced by private enforcement agents. Mediation is mandatory for smaller commercial claims. For larger matters, arbitration under the 2006 UNCITRAL-based law is common. Early asset research helps counsel judge whether enforcement is worth pursuing.

      Are private investigators licensed in North Macedonia?

      Detective activity is understood to be subject to specific legislation and authorisation, but we have not verified the current text, so the position is confirmed with local counsel for each matter. TDSC does not hold a Macedonian licence; where licensed local work is needed, it coordinates appropriately licensed local professionals and keeps all enquiries within data protection and criminal law limits.
