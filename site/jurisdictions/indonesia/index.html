      Overview

      Indonesia at a Glance

      Indonesia is the largest economy in Southeast Asia and an archipelago of several thousand inhabited islands, with business activity concentrated in Jakarta but material operations spread across Java, Sumatra, Kalimantan, Sulawesi and beyond. Its legal system is based on civil law, judges are not bound by precedent, and many laws are open to varying interpretation, so documentary findings need careful framing. Companies are established through the Ministry of Law, and business licensing runs through the risk-based Online Single Submission system. The Personal Data Protection Law No. 27 of 2022 has applied in full since 17 October 2024, and corporations must identify their beneficial owners under Presidential Regulation No. 13 of 2018.

      Organisations commission work involving Indonesia because of its weight in natural resources, palm oil, nickel and other minerals, manufacturing, digital services and infrastructure, and because many regional groups hold Indonesian assets through layered holding structures. Recurring exposures include nominee shareholding arrangements, licensing and land-title irregularities, related-party transactions between family-controlled conglomerates, and dealings with officials in permitting and procurement. Foreign investors, lenders, insurers and arbitration counsel also regularly need reliable background on Indonesian partners, and asset tracing to support enforcement, where companies report that enforcing international arbitration awards can take years.

      Law & Regulation

      What applies before any work touching Indonesia begins

      Legal system

      Indonesia's legal system is based on civil law, with judges not bound by precedent. The court system includes District Courts, High Courts, the Supreme Court and a Constitutional Court, and Commercial Courts handle bankruptcy and suspension-of-payment matters and appeals from competition authority decisions. Arbitration is governed by Law No. 30 of 1999 on Arbitration and Alternative Dispute Resolution; the Indonesian National Arbitration Body (BANI), established by the Indonesian Chamber of Commerce and Industry in 1977, administers domestic and other arbitrations.

      Data protection

      Law No. 27 of 2022 on Personal Data Protection (PDP Law) · Regulator: Ministry of Communications and Digital Affairs (Komdigi), pending establishment of the PDP Agency

      The PDP Law, largely modelled on the GDPR, became fully enforceable on 17 October 2024. Processing requires one of six bases, including consent, legal obligation and legitimate interests, which is the basis most relevant to investigative work. Cross-border transfers are permitted to countries with equal or higher protection, otherwise under binding safeguards or with consent. Breaches must be notified in writing within 72 hours. The dedicated PDP Agency had not yet been formed as of the latest available guidance, and implementing regulations remain pending. Government Regulation 71/2019 and financial-sector rules add data-location conditions for some operators.

      Private investigators

      We have not identified a dedicated licensing regime for private investigators in Indonesia in the sources reviewed. Private security businesses are subject to police oversight, but how that framework applies to investigative services should be confirmed with Indonesian counsel for each matter. In practice, engagements are scoped so that documentary research relies on lawful public sources, and any interviews, site visits or field observation are carried out by appropriately qualified local professionals under counsel's guidance, with particular care given to personal data, recording, trespass and any contact with officials.

      Corporate & public records

      Limited liability companies are established through the Ministry of Law, whose general legal administration directorate (AHU) maintains company data, and business identity numbers and permits are issued through the Online Single Submission system. Corporations must disclose their beneficial owners under Presidential Regulation No. 13 of 2018, and Minister of Law Regulation No. 2 of 2025 now governs verification and supervision of that information. Land is registered by the National Land Agency, where registration is strong but not conclusive evidence of ownership. Public access to company and beneficial ownership data should be confirmed for each matter.

      Anti-corruption & AML

      The Corruption Eradication Commission (KPK), established in 2002, investigates and prosecutes corruption, and its charter was revised by Law No. 19 of 2019, which reduced its independence; the Attorney General's Office also prosecutes corruption cases. The core offences are set out in Law No. 31 of 1999 as amended by Law No. 20 of 2001. Money laundering is addressed by Law No. 8 of 2010, with the Financial Transaction Reports and Analysis Centre (PPATK) acting as the financial intelligence unit.

      Typical Matters

      Where Investigative Work Involving Indonesia Concentrates

      01

      Nominee and beneficial ownership

      Analysis of who actually controls an Indonesian company where registered shareholders may be nominees, combining Ministry of Law corporate data, beneficial ownership disclosures where accessible, litigation records and Indonesian-language media to map family and business relationships behind a counterparty.

      02

      Resource and plantation partners

      Due diligence on mining, nickel processing, palm oil and forestry partners, testing licences, land rights, community or environmental disputes and political connections, for investors and buyers whose own supply-chain and anti-corruption obligations reach into Indonesian operations.

      03

      Asset tracing for award enforcement

      Identification of companies, shareholdings, land and other assets connected to an Indonesian respondent or judgment debtor, supporting counsel deciding where and how to enforce, given that enforcement of international arbitration awards in Indonesia can be slow.

      04

      Permitting and procurement exposure

      Reviews of agents, consultants and joint-venture partners involved in licensing, customs or government procurement, identifying links to officials and red flags relevant to the KPK's areas of focus and to foreign anti-bribery laws that apply to the client.

      05

      Distributor and employee fraud

      Support to multinationals and their counsel on diversion, kickbacks and conflicts of interest in Indonesian distribution networks and subsidiaries, structured around the PDP Law's legitimate-interest basis and with interviews conducted locally under Indonesian counsel's direction.

      Engagement Standard

      How TDSC Scopes Matters Touching Indonesia

      TDSC scopes Indonesian matters from Nashville by defining the client's purpose and lawful basis under the PDP Law, usually legitimate interests, and planning whether findings will leave Indonesia and under which transfer mechanism. Research draws on Ministry of Law corporate data, OSS licensing information, court decisions, regulatory announcements and Indonesian-language national and regional media, reviewed by Indonesian-speaking analysts. Because we have not identified a dedicated investigator licensing regime, any local enquiries, interviews or site visits are scoped with Indonesian counsel and carried out by qualified local professionals. TDSC does not approach officials for information, obtain non-public records or use pretexting, and keeps case data under restricted access with defined retention.

      TDSC has no office in Indonesia. Engagements are directed from Nashville, Tennessee; where work on the ground is needed, it is performed by appropriately licensed local professionals under a written scope. TDSC does not provide legal advice.

      Questions

      Indonesia: Frequently Asked Questions

      Is the PDP Law being enforced if the PDP Agency does not yet exist?

      The PDP Law's transition period ended on 17 October 2024, so controllers and processors must comply. Until the PDP Agency is formed, the Ministry of Communications and Digital Affairs oversees personal data processed through electronic systems, and sector regulators such as the financial services authority may also act. Implementing regulations are still pending.

      Can the real owners of an Indonesian company be identified?

      Corporations must identify and report their beneficial owners under Presidential Regulation No. 13 of 2018, and a 2025 Minister of Law regulation governs verification and supervision of that data. Nominee arrangements remain a practical risk, so ownership work combines corporate records with litigation, media and relationship analysis rather than relying on filings alone.

      Are private investigators licensed in Indonesia?

      We have not identified a dedicated licensing regime for private investigators in the sources reviewed. That does not mean field work is unregulated: data protection, criminal and property laws still apply, and security businesses face police oversight. TDSC scopes any local activity with Indonesian counsel and uses appropriately qualified local professionals.

      Do Indonesian land records show who owns property?

      Land is registered by the National Land Agency, which has introduced electronic registration under Government Regulation No. 18 of 2021. Registration is treated as strong but not conclusive evidence of ownership, and a share of titles has been found improperly validated, so land findings are presented with appropriate caveats and corroboration.

      Which bodies handle corruption and money laundering?

      The Corruption Eradication Commission (KPK) and the Attorney General's Office investigate and prosecute corruption, while PPATK is the financial intelligence unit under the money laundering law, Law No. 8 of 2010. TDSC can compile documentary findings for counsel assessing exposure but does not give legal advice or engage with authorities on a client's behalf.
