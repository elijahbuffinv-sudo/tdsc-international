      Central African Republic

      Investigations Touching Central African Republic

      Matters involving the Central African Republic tend to centre on mining, diamonds, timber, humanitarian contracting and the counterparties operating in a fragile security environment. Investigative consulting here means sanctions-aware due diligence, careful use of limited OHADA records and coordination with qualified professionals in Bangui.

      Overview

      Central African Republic at a Glance

      The Central African Republic is a landlocked civil law state whose business law follows the OHADA uniform acts shared across much of francophone Africa, and which belongs to the CEMAC monetary and economic community. Formal information infrastructure is thin. The commercial register is held at the Tribunal de Commerce de Bangui, appears to be paper-based, and has no confirmed online search. Large parts of the country, particularly in the north-east, have been contested by armed groups, and foreign security actors have been linked to mining operations. For investigations this means public records answer only part of the question, field inquiries carry real safety and legal constraints, and conclusions must often rest on corroboration from several independent sources.

      Organisations commission work involving the Central African Republic most often because of exposure to its extractive sectors. Diamonds, gold and timber are the principal exports of interest, and the Kimberley Process lifted its embargo on Central African rough diamond exports in November 2024 after more than a decade, renewing buyer and refiner interest along with concerns about provenance. Other exposures arise for donors, NGOs and contractors operating in humanitarian and stabilisation programmes, for lenders and traders handling regional supply chains through Cameroon and the Republic of the Congo, and for compliance teams screening counterparties with possible links to sanctioned networks, including companies designated by the US Treasury in 2024 for links to the Wagner Group.

      Background research on Central African companies, shareholders and directors where the risk is a connection to designated persons, including mining companies designated by the US Treasury in 2024 for links to the Wagner Group, or to UN-listed individuals.

      Legal Environment

      How the law shapes investigative work involving Central African Republic

      Legal system

      The Central African Republic has a civil law system based on the French model. Commercial matters are governed by the OHADA uniform acts, including those on commercial companies and general commercial law, and commercial disputes are heard at first instance by the commercial court in Bangui, which also keeps the commercial register. The higher courts include the Court of Cassation and the Council of State, and OHADA matters may go on final appeal to the Common Court of Justice and Arbitration (CCJA) in Abidjan. Arbitration is governed by the OHADA Uniform Act on Arbitration of 23 November 2017. Foreign judgments require exequatur.

      Data protection

      Law No. 24.001 on the protection of personal data (2024) · Regulator: Independent administrative authority to be established under Law No. 24.001; the Ministry of Digital Economy, Posts and Telecommunications oversees the law in the interim

      Law No. 24.001, adopted in 2024, is the country's first comprehensive data protection law. Processing requires a lawful basis such as consent, contract, legal obligation, public interest or legitimate interests, and sensitive data, including political opinions, religious beliefs and health information, is generally prohibited subject to exceptions such as legal claims. Transfers abroad are permitted to states offering a similar level of protection; transfers within CEMAC and ECCAS must be necessary for the recipient's legitimate functions, and other transfers rely on derogations or authorisation of the authority where adequate safeguards such as contractual clauses exist. Whether the supervisory authority is fully operational as of 2026 has not been confirmed.

      Private investigators

      We have not identified a dedicated licensing regime for private investigators or detective agencies in the Central African Republic, and we have not verified the rules that may apply to private security firms. TDSC therefore treats the question as open in each engagement and scopes any in-country work with local counsel in Bangui, who confirm whether licensing, registration or authorisation applies before a local professional is instructed. Independent of licensing, field inquiries are constrained by the security situation, by Law No. 24.001 on personal data and by criminal law. TDSC does not commission surveillance, recording or contact with officials in the country without counsel's advice.

      Corporate & public records

      Companies, branches and sole traders are registered in the Registre du Commerce et du Crédit Mobilier (RCCM), kept by the Tribunal de Commerce de Bangui under Ministry of Justice supervision. No reliable online search has been confirmed; extracts are reported to be issued on paper in French after an in-person request, and may show name, RCCM number, legal form, registered address, activity, share capital and directors or partners. Data can be incomplete or out of date. No public beneficial ownership register has been identified. Mining and forestry concession information, Kimberley Process data and UN Panel of Experts reports are often more informative than the registry.

      Anti-corruption & AML

      The Central African Republic has ratified the UN Convention against Corruption and is not a party to the OECD Anti-Bribery Convention. We have not verified the current domestic anti-corruption statute or the anti-money laundering framework and enforcement bodies for this page; as a CEMAC member the country is expected to apply regional AML rules, but specifics are being confirmed. Engagements that depend on these rules are scoped with local counsel.

      How TDSC Scopes Matters Touching Central African Republic

      TDSC handles matters touching the Central African Republic from Nashville as scoped consulting engagements, beginning with a sanctions screen of the client, the subjects and the purpose of the work. As a US company, TDSC does not accept engagements that would breach US or other applicable sanctions, and it will decline or limit work where designated persons are involved. Research relies on lawful sources: RCCM extracts obtained through Bangui counsel, concession and export data, Kimberley Process material, UN reporting and regional media. Any in-country work is coordinated with local counsel and appropriately qualified local professionals after a security and legal assessment. TDSC does not seek non-public government records. Personal data is handled with Law No. 24.001 and its transfer rules in mind.

      TDSC has no office in Central African Republic. Engagements are directed from Nashville, Tennessee; where work on the ground is needed, it is performed by appropriately licensed local professionals under a written scope. TDSC does not provide legal advice.

      Questions

      Central African Republic: Frequently Asked Questions

      Can a Central African company search be run online?

      We have not confirmed any reliable online search of the RCCM. Extracts are reported to be issued on paper in French after a request at the Tribunal de Commerce de Bangui, usually through a local lawyer. Records can be incomplete or outdated, so TDSC corroborates registry information with concession records, trade data and other public sources before relying on it.

      Does the end of the Kimberley Process embargo remove diamond risk?

      No. The Kimberley Process lifted its embargo on Central African rough diamond exports in November 2024, with reporting obligations to follow in 2025. Civil society groups criticised the decision as premature, and insecurity persists in mining regions. Buyers still need provenance work on exporters and intermediaries and screening for links to armed groups or sanctioned networks.

      Is there a data protection law in the Central African Republic?

      Yes. Law No. 24.001, adopted in 2024, sets lawful bases for processing, restricts sensitive data and controls transfers abroad, with special rules for transfers within CEMAC and ECCAS. It provides for an independent supervisory authority; whether that authority is fully operational as of 2026 has not been confirmed, so TDSC applies the law's principles directly.

      How does TDSC handle sanctions questions in Central African matters?

      Every matter is screened before acceptance. The US Treasury has designated companies operating in the country's mining sector for links to the Wagner Group, and UN measures also apply to certain individuals. TDSC will not act for, or facilitate dealings with, designated persons and narrows or declines work where a lawful scope cannot be defined with counsel.

      Can investigators work on the ground outside Bangui?

      Only after a specific security and legal assessment. Large areas have been contested by armed groups, and access can change quickly. Where field inquiries are justified, TDSC coordinates with local counsel and appropriately qualified local professionals, and often relies instead on remote research, documents obtained through counsel and interviews conducted in safer locations.
