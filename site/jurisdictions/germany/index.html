      Overview

      Germany at a Glance

      Germany is a federal civil-law state with sixteen Länder, a deep base of family-owned Mittelstand companies and a court system that is both thorough and procedurally formal. Its corporate records are unusually accessible: since August 2022 the joint register portal of the states has allowed free online retrieval of Handelsregister entries and filed documents. Personal data, by contrast, is tightly protected under the GDPR, the Federal Data Protection Act (BDSG) and an active network of state supervisory authorities. Investigative work in Germany therefore rewards careful use of public sources, documented legal bases for any processing of personal data, and early coordination with German counsel on how findings may later be used in proceedings.

      Organisations commission work touching Germany because of its central place in European manufacturing, automotive, chemicals, engineering and financial services, and because German companies sit in supply chains worldwide. Typical exposures include acquiring or partnering with privately held Mittelstand businesses whose ownership runs through GmbH and GmbH and Co. KG structures, distributor and agent relationships in Central and Eastern Europe managed from Germany, suspected commercial bribery or procurement fraud, and enforcing judgments or arbitral awards against German assets. Supply chain due diligence obligations under German law and forthcoming EU rules also prompt counterparties abroad to verify their German partners and customers.

      Jurisdictional Framework

      The rules that govern lawful inquiry in Germany

      Legal system

      Germany has a civil-law system built on codified law, principally the Civil Code (BGB), the Commercial Code (HGB), the Code of Civil Procedure (ZPO) and the Criminal Code (StGB). Commercial disputes are heard by the Regional Courts (Landgerichte), many with chambers for commercial matters, with appeals to the Higher Regional Courts and the Federal Court of Justice. The Commercial Courts Act, in force since 1 April 2025, allows states to establish Commercial Courts at Higher Regional Court level and permits English-language proceedings by party agreement. Arbitration is commonly administered by the German Arbitration Institute (DIS).

      Data protection

      General Data Protection Regulation (EU) 2016/679 and Federal Data Protection Act (BDSG, 2018) · Regulator: Data protection authorities of the 16 Länder for private-sector bodies; Federal Commissioner for Data Protection and Freedom of Information (BfDI) for federal bodies and telecom and postal providers

      Any investigative processing of personal data needs a documented lawful basis under the GDPR, usually legitimate interests or the establishment and defence of legal claims, supported by a balancing assessment. Employee investigations are stricter: German rules require a documented, factual suspicion of an offence committed in the employment relationship, and the measures must be necessary and proportionate, with Federal Labour Court case law setting the practical limits. Works councils may have co-determination rights over monitoring. Transfers of personal data to the United States or other third countries require a GDPR transfer mechanism. Telecommunications secrecy is protected by criminal law.

      Private investigators

      Germany has no dedicated licensing regime for private investigators. A detective agency (Detektei) needs no statutory permit to be established but must register a trade (Gewerbeanmeldung) with the local trade office, and detective and information-agency work is classed under section 38 of the Trade Regulation Act (GewO) as a trade requiring special supervision, which triggers a reliability check including a police certificate of conduct. Guarding activity requires a separate permit under section 34a GewO. Investigators have no special powers: covert recording of private speech, interception of communications and unauthorised access to data are criminal offences, and surveillance must remain proportionate and lawful under data protection rules.

      Corporate & public records

      The commercial register (Handelsregister) is kept by local courts and searchable free of charge through the joint register portal of the states, which since August 2022 also provides filed documents such as articles of association and, for GmbHs, shareholder lists. Annual financial statements are published in the Company Register (Unternehmensregister). Following the Court of Justice of the European Union's judgment of 22 November 2022, the Transparency Register of beneficial owners (Transparenzregister) is no longer open to the general public; access requires a demonstrated legitimate interest. Land registers (Grundbuch) are available only to those showing a legitimate interest.

      Anti-corruption & AML

      Bribery of public officials is criminalised under sections 331 to 335a of the Criminal Code (StGB), which extend to foreign and EU officials, and commercial bribery under sections 299 and 300, with healthcare-specific offences in sections 299a and 299b. Companies are fined under section 30 of the Administrative Offences Act (OWiG), and managers face liability for supervisory failures under section 130. The Money Laundering Act (GwG) governs AML duties. Enforcement rests with state public prosecutors, several with specialised anti-corruption units, and the police, with the Financial Intelligence Unit at the customs administration receiving suspicious transaction reports.

      Engagement Standard

      How TDSC Scopes Matters Touching Germany

      TDSC begins every German matter with a jurisdictional review: the purpose of the inquiry, the lawful basis for any processing of personal data, whether employees or a works council are involved, and how findings may be used in German or foreign proceedings. Research draws first on the Handelsregister, Unternehmensregister, insolvency announcements, court publications and German-language media. Where work must be done on the ground, such as discreet inquiries or document retrieval from local courts, TDSC coordinates registered German professionals and works alongside the client's German counsel, who advises on legal questions. Personal data is minimised, transferred only under an appropriate GDPR mechanism and retained no longer than the engagement requires. TDSC does not seek access to restricted registers without a legitimate interest established through proper channels.

      TDSC has no office in Germany. Engagements are directed from Nashville, Tennessee; where work on the ground is needed, it is performed by appropriately licensed local professionals under a written scope. TDSC does not provide legal advice.

      Questions

      Germany: Frequently Asked Questions

      Can beneficial ownership of a German company still be confirmed after the Transparenzregister closed to the public?

      Often, yes, through other lawful routes. GmbH shareholder lists filed with the Handelsregister are freely available online and frequently reveal ownership chains. The Transparenzregister itself now requires a demonstrated legitimate interest, so where it matters an application is made through proper channels, typically with German counsel, rather than assumed to be available.

      Is surveillance of an employee in Germany permitted during an internal investigation?

      Only within narrow limits. German law and labour court practice require a documented, concrete suspicion of a serious breach or offence, proportionate measures and no less intrusive alternative. Covert recording of private conversations is a criminal offence. Works council rights may apply. We scope any such step with the employer's German employment counsel before anything is done.

      Do German private investigators hold a licence?

      Germany does not license private investigators as a profession. Agencies register a trade with the local authority and undergo a reliability check because detective work is a supervised trade. Because the regime does not certify competence, TDSC selects local professionals on track record, professional association membership and references, and sets out lawful methods in writing.

      Can findings be presented in English before a German court?

      Since 1 April 2025, states may establish Commercial Courts where parties can agree to English-language proceedings, and some states have done so. In ordinary proceedings, German remains the court language and translations are usually required. We prepare reports with this in mind and coordinate translation with counsel.

      What public sources are most useful for a German counterparty check?

      The Handelsregister and its filed documents, published annual financial statements in the Unternehmensregister, insolvency announcements, the Federal Gazette, court decisions published by the states and German-language regional press. Together these usually establish identity, ownership, management history, financial filing compliance and reputational issues without touching restricted data.
