      Israel: Investigative Consulting

      Investigative consulting for matters touching Israel means working with a licensed investigator profession, a privacy law strengthened by Amendment 13 in 2025, Hebrew-language registry and court records, and a technology-heavy economy where founders, investors and intellectual property often move between Israel and the United States.

      Overview

      Israel at a Glance

      Israel has a mixed legal system derived from common law, with adversarial procedure, no juries and a body of Basic Laws given constitutional weight, including Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty (1992), which protects privacy. Company law is detailed, corporate records are accessible and courts publish many decisions. Privacy is taken seriously: the Protection of Privacy Law, 1981, was significantly amended by Amendment 13, in force since 14 August 2025, giving the Privacy Protection Authority criminal investigation and monetary sanction powers. Private investigators are a regulated profession, and Israeli authorities have pursued investigators who obtained personal data from databases through impersonation, so method and source matter.

      Organisations commission work involving Israel because of its concentration of technology companies, research and development centres and venture capital, and its deep commercial ties with the United States, where US firms account for nearly two-thirds of more than 300 multinational R&D centres in Israel, according to the US State Department. Typical exposures include due diligence on start-ups, founders and investors before funding rounds or acquisitions, disputes over intellectual property and employee mobility between competitors, defence and dual-use supply-chain questions, cyber incidents, and tracing assets or business interests of counterparties who operate across Israeli and US entities.

      Typical Matters

      Where Investigative Work Involving Israel Concentrates

      01

      Technology investment due diligence

      Background research on Israeli start-ups, founders, key engineers and investors before funding rounds, acquisitions or US listings, covering corporate history, prior ventures, litigation, regulatory actions and Hebrew-language media, with attention to undisclosed related parties, prior company failures and claims over ownership of core technology.

      02

      IP and employee mobility disputes

      Supporting counsel where engineers or teams leave for a competitor or found a rival company, by building public-record and open-source chronologies of new entities, patent filings, product launches and hiring, while leaving evidence-gathering involving individuals to licensed Israeli investigators and court procedures.

      03

      Defence and dual-use supply chains

      Verifying Israeli suppliers, integrators and intermediaries in defence, security and dual-use technology chains, including ownership, affiliations, export-control history and reputational issues, to support US compliance programmes and procurement decisions without accessing restricted or government sources.

      04

      Cross-border asset research

      Mapping Israeli companies, directorships, shareholdings and registered charges linked to a judgment debtor or counterparty who operates through both Israeli and US entities, using Corporations Authority extracts and public filings, to inform counsel on recovery strategy and the value of Israeli enforcement proceedings.

      05

      Cyber incident and fraud support

      Assisting counsel and incident responders after fraud, business email compromise or data incidents that involve Israeli vendors, payment intermediaries or technology providers, by verifying entities and individuals as publicly recorded and documenting facts for notification, insurance and recovery decisions under Israeli and US privacy rules.

      Jurisdictional Framework

      The rules that govern lawful inquiry in Israel

      Legal system

      Israel's legal system is mixed, rooted in common law with civil law influences and no jury trials. Thirty magistrates' courts hear smaller civil claims; six district courts hear larger civil disputes, company and partnership matters and appeals; and the Supreme Court in Jerusalem is the final appellate court and also sits as the High Court of Justice. Labour courts and religious courts operate separately. Arbitration is governed by the Arbitration Law of 1968, last amended in 2008. Proceedings are conducted in Hebrew.

      Data protection

      Protection of Privacy Law, 5741-1981, as amended by Amendment 13 (in force 14 August 2025), with the Privacy Protection (Data Security) Regulations, 5777-2017, and transfer regulations of 2001 and 2023 · Regulator: Privacy Protection Authority (PPA), Ministry of Justice

      The Protection of Privacy Law protects against infringement of privacy, including surveillance, unauthorised use of personal information and publication of private matters, and regulates databases. Amendment 13 introduced the concept of especially sensitive data, data protection officer duties, narrower database registration and stronger enforcement powers for the Privacy Protection Authority. The 2017 Data Security Regulations set security obligations for databases. Transfers abroad are permitted where the destination provides comparable protection or a listed condition, such as consent, applies, and require contractual commitments. Israel holds an EU adequacy decision, reconfirmed in January 2024, and 2023 regulations add duties for data received from the European Economic Area.

      Private investigators

      Private investigation in Israel is a licensed profession under the Private Investigators and Security Services Law, 5732-1972, with licensing administered by the state; acting as a private investigator without a licence is an offence. Licensed investigators remain bound by the Protection of Privacy Law, which treats surveillance, harassment and unauthorised use of personal information as privacy infringements, and by restrictions on intercepting communications. Israeli authorities have investigated private investigators suspected of obtaining personal data from insurer and other databases by impersonation, which reflects the enforcement risk around pretexting. Foreign firms should coordinate any Israeli fieldwork through licensed local investigators and counsel.

      Corporate & public records

      Companies, partnerships and non-profit associations are registered with the Israeli Corporations Authority in the Ministry of Justice, which maintains the Registrar of Companies. Company extracts showing registration details, directors, shareholders and registered charges are available to the public for a fee, and annual reports and changes are filed with the Registrar. Listed companies publish filings through the Israel Securities Authority's MAGNA system and the Tel Aviv Stock Exchange's MAYA system. Land registration records are kept by the Land Registry (Tabu). Open Ownership records no beneficial ownership register commitment for Israel; we have not identified a public beneficial ownership register.

      Anti-corruption & AML

      Bribery of domestic officials and, since 2008, foreign public officials is criminalised under the Penal Law, 5737-1977, and Israel is a party to the OECD Anti-Bribery Convention. Money laundering is governed by the Prohibition on Money Laundering Law, 5760-2000, with the Israel Money Laundering and Terror Financing Prohibition Authority as the financial intelligence unit. The Israel Police, the State Attorney's Office, the Attorney General, the Israel Securities Authority and the State Comptroller are central to enforcement and oversight.

      Engagement Standard

      How TDSC Scopes Matters Touching Israel

      TDSC scopes Israel matters from Nashville with two constraints in view: private investigation in Israel is a licensed activity, and the Protection of Privacy Law, strengthened by Amendment 13, makes surveillance and misuse of personal information actionable. TDSC's own work relies on lawful sources: Corporations Authority extracts, securities filings, published court decisions, patent records and Hebrew-language media read by Hebrew-literate researchers. Where a matter needs inquiries or observation in Israel, TDSC coordinates licensed Israeli investigators and works with the client's Israeli counsel, who direct anything intended as evidence. Personal data is minimised and documented, and data originating in the European Economic Area is handled with the additional Israeli requirements in mind.

      TDSC has no office in Israel. Engagements are directed from Nashville, Tennessee; where work on the ground is needed, it is performed by appropriately licensed local professionals under a written scope. TDSC does not provide legal advice.

      Questions

      Israel: Frequently Asked Questions

      Do private investigators need a licence in Israel?

      Yes. Private investigation is a licensed profession under the Private Investigators and Security Services Law, 5732-1972, and working as an investigator without a licence is an offence. TDSC does not hold an Israeli licence and does not conduct investigative fieldwork in Israel itself. Where on-the-ground inquiries are needed, TDSC coordinates licensed Israeli investigators and the client's Israeli counsel.

      How did Amendment 13 change privacy obligations in Israel?

      Amendment 13 to the Protection of Privacy Law took effect on 14 August 2025. It introduced especially sensitive data as a category, required many organisations to appoint data protection officers, narrowed database registration and gave the Privacy Protection Authority criminal investigation and monetary sanction powers. For investigations, it raises the stakes for collecting and holding personal data without a clear lawful basis.

      What information is available on an Israeli company?

      Extracts from the Registrar of Companies at the Israeli Corporations Authority show the company's registration details, directors, shareholders and registered charges, and are available to the public for a fee. Listed companies file reports through the MAGNA and MAYA systems. Published court decisions and Hebrew-language business press often add context on disputes, insolvency and management changes.

      Can evidence gathered in Israel be used in US litigation?

      Possibly, but admissibility depends on how it was obtained. Material gathered in breach of the Protection of Privacy Law or Israeli interception restrictions may expose the client and investigators to liability in Israel and be challenged in US proceedings. TDSC recommends that Israeli and US counsel agree the method in advance, and that any fieldwork be performed by licensed Israeli investigators.

      Is pretexting used to obtain information in Israel?

      TDSC does not use pretexting in any jurisdiction. In Israel, authorities have investigated private investigators suspected of obtaining personal information from insurers and other databases by impersonating others, reflecting active enforcement of privacy law. Research for TDSC engagements is limited to lawful public sources and to information obtained through licensed professionals and counsel acting within Israeli law.
