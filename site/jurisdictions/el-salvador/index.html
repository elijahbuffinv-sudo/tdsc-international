      Republic of El Salvador

      El Salvador: Investigative Consulting

      In El Salvador, investigative consulting means checking counterparties in a dollarised economy that is courting nearshoring, digital asset businesses and tourism. That work has to account for a changing data protection framework, concerns about judicial independence and limited transparency over public spending.

      Overview

      El Salvador at a Glance

      El Salvador has about 6.4 million people and uses the US dollar as legal tender. Bitcoin was also made legal tender in 2021. The country has a civil law system with a Commercial Code and specialised commercial courts. Investors have historically described litigation as slow and costly. The removal of the Constitutional Chamber justices and the Attorney General in May 2021 raised continuing questions about judicial independence. Since March 2022 a state of exception has been in force against gangs, which has sharply reduced violent crime and affects how fieldwork is planned. Companies can now be formed online, including as simplified joint-stock companies, a form introduced in December 2023.

      Organisations commission work involving El Salvador because of growing interest in nearshoring in textiles and apparel, light manufacturing, pharmaceuticals, business process outsourcing and technology services, along with tourism and energy investment. The digital asset framework has attracted new issuers and service providers, many of them recently formed and owned from abroad. Typical needs include verifying manufacturing and outsourcing partners, researching the background and ownership of digital asset counterparties, checking politically exposed persons in public procurement, where information on some purchases is not published, and tracing assets for disputes with links to the large Salvadoran diaspora in the United States.

      Legal Environment

      How the law shapes investigative work involving El Salvador

      Legal system

      El Salvador has a civil law system. Commercial matters are governed by the Commercial Code and the Code of Civil and Commercial Procedure, and specialised commercial courts hear them. The judiciary consists of the Supreme Court of Justice, including its Constitutional Chamber, the chambers of second instance, the courts of first instance and the justices of the peace. Arbitration is available under 2002 legislation that allows private arbitration centres, the main one being the Mediation and Arbitration Centre of the Chamber of Commerce and Industry. A 2009 amendment allows certain arbitral rulings to be appealed to the courts.

      Data protection

      No comprehensive data protection law confirmed in force (a 2021 bill was vetoed); sectoral rules including the Credit History Law (as amended 2022) · Regulator: No general data protection authority confirmed; Institute of Access to Public Information for certain public-sector data

      As of DLA Piper's January 2024 update, El Salvador had no comprehensive personal data protection law in force. A bill approved in 2021 was vetoed and not re-adopted. We have not verified whether later legislation has taken effect. Protection comes from scattered rules that generally require the data subject's written authorisation to transfer their data, and from the constitutional right to privacy. Amendments to the Credit History Law in 2022 require credit data to be stored in El Salvador. Investigative work should rely on public sources, documented purposes and the client's home-jurisdiction rules.

      Private investigators

      We have not confirmed from sources we could open whether El Salvador has a specific licensing regime for private investigators, or how private security legislation applies to investigative services. We therefore scope engagements cautiously. Interception of communications, unauthorised access to computer systems and violations of privacy are criminal matters. Records held by banks, the tax authority and the state are not available to private inquirers. Because the state of exception has expanded police powers and detentions, any visible local inquiry needs careful planning with Salvadoran counsel and appropriately qualified local professionals.

      Corporate & public records

      Companies are registered in the Commercial Registry run by the Centro Nacional de Registros (CNR), which also runs the property registry. Since online formation through the Miempresa portal was introduced, companies can be formed within days, and simplified joint-stock companies need only one US dollar of capital. Registry filings generally include incorporation deeds, administrators' appointments and amendments, and certifications can be ordered. An OECD Global Forum review found that beneficial ownership information needed improvement, and we have not confirmed a public beneficial ownership register. Real property records at the CNR are an established source for asset research.

      Anti-corruption & AML

      The Money Laundering Law requires financial institutions to report suspicious transactions to the Financial Investigation Unit (UIF) of the Attorney General's Office (Fiscalía General de la República), which also prosecutes corruption. The Court of Accounts audits public bodies and publishes its results, but its ability to audit subsidiaries of state-owned enterprises has been limited. The Public Procurement Law governs government purchases. Information on purchases made through the BOLPROS exchange is not public. El Salvador ranks low on international perception indices.

      Engagement Standard

      How TDSC Scopes Matters Touching El Salvador

      TDSC handles El Salvador matters from Nashville. We start with a jurisdictional review: what the CNR commercial and property registries hold, what court and Court of Accounts records are available, and what can only be obtained through counsel or a court order. Because the data protection framework is in flux, we confirm the current position with Salvadoran counsel and apply data minimisation and documented purposes regardless. For digital asset and nearshoring counterparties we usually research the founders' home jurisdictions alongside El Salvador. Where certified records, site visits or interviews are needed, we coordinate qualified local professionals. Under the state of exception, we keep local inquiries low-profile and avoid any activity that could put sources at risk.

      TDSC has no office in El Salvador. Engagements are directed from Nashville, Tennessee; where work on the ground is needed, it is performed by appropriately licensed local professionals under a written scope. TDSC does not provide legal advice.

      Typical Matters

      Where Investigative Work Involving El Salvador Concentrates

      01

      Nearshoring partner verification

      Ownership, operating history and labour compliance research on apparel, light manufacturing, medical device and outsourcing partners. It supports US buyers relocating supply chains to Central America and checks for undisclosed related parties or prior insolvencies.

      02

      Digital asset counterparty research

      Background, ownership and regulatory history research on recently formed digital asset issuers, exchanges and service providers registered under El Salvador's digital asset framework. Many are owned from abroad, so the research draws on records in the founders' home jurisdictions.

      03

      Procurement and PEP exposure

      Identifying links between counterparties and politically exposed persons in public contracting. Information on some purchases, including those made through BOLPROS, is not published, and audit coverage of state-owned enterprise subsidiaries is limited.

      04

      Diaspora-linked asset tracing

      Tracing property, companies and funds where disputes, fraud or estates connect the United States and El Salvador, using CNR commercial and property registries alongside US records to locate assets that may be available for enforcement.

      05

      Tourism and energy investments

      Due diligence on developers, landholders and partners in coastal tourism zones and energy projects, including title history, permitting disputes and the track record of local sponsors before investors or lenders commit.

      Questions

      El Salvador: Frequently Asked Questions

      What can be learned from the CNR Commercial Registry?

      The CNR registry records incorporation deeds, amendments and appointments of administrators, and certifications can be ordered. Because companies, including SAS entities, can now be formed quickly and with minimal capital, we compare registry data with tax, litigation, media and foreign records to judge whether an entity has real operations and who controls it.

      Does El Salvador have a data protection law?

      As of DLA Piper's January 2024 update, no comprehensive law was in force after a 2021 bill was vetoed. Sectoral rules apply, such as the data localisation requirement for credit information. We check the current position with Salvadoran counsel at the start of each engagement because legislative changes may have occurred, and we handle personal data conservatively in the meantime.

      How does the state of exception affect investigations?

      Since March 2022, emergency measures have expanded police powers and led to mass detentions. Visible inquiries in some communities can expose sources or the people being interviewed to risk. We favour documentary research, keep local engagement low-profile, and scope any interviews with counsel and experienced local professionals.

      Can we identify the people behind a Salvadoran digital asset company?

      Often in part. Registry filings name administrators and incorporation partners, and registration with the National Digital Assets Commission is a further reference point. Many such companies are owned from abroad, so we also research corporate, regulatory and litigation records in the founders' home jurisdictions, and we report clearly what remains unverified.

      Is arbitration effective for disputes in El Salvador?

      Arbitration is available through private centres such as the Chamber of Commerce and Industry's centre, but a 2009 amendment allows certain rulings to be appealed to the courts, and enforcement can take time. That is a matter for counsel. We support it with counterparty facts and asset identification.
