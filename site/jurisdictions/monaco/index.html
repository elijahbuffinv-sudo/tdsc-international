      Overview

      Monaco at a Glance

      Monaco is a sovereign principality with a civil law tradition closely aligned with French law, its own codes and courts, and a highest civil and criminal court, the Cour de révision, staffed by judges drawn from France. Its institutions are small and its business community closely connected. Personal data is governed by a GDPR-comparable statute adopted in December 2024, under which processing for surveillance or concerning suspected offences requires prior authorisation from the data protection authority, the CCIN. That rule has direct consequences for investigative work. Commercial activity is concentrated in finance, insurance, real estate, wholesale trade and services, and much of what investigators need sits with banks, family offices, trust and corporate service providers, and notaries rather than in public registers.

      Organisations commission work involving Monaco because it is home to substantial private wealth, a concentrated wealth management and banking sector, and high-value real estate, and because Monaco-resident individuals and companies appear frequently in cross-border disputes, divorces, insolvencies and enforcement proceedings. Typical needs include asset and lifestyle research supporting litigation, due diligence on individuals seeking investment or partnership, reputational screening of residents and their companies, and support for counsel on fraud and breach of trust claims. Matters are often linked to France, Italy, Switzerland, the United Kingdom and offshore centres, so investigative scoping must address several legal systems together.

      Law & Regulation

      What applies before any work touching Monaco begins

      Legal system

      Monaco has a civil law system closely modelled on French law, with its own Civil Code, Commercial Code and procedural codes. Commercial disputes are heard at first instance by the Tribunal de première instance, with appeal to the Cour d'appel and final review on points of law by the Cour de révision, which hears civil and criminal matters and is staffed by judges from France. The Tribunal suprême exercises constitutional and administrative review under the 1962 Constitution. We have not identified a specialised commercial court or a dedicated local arbitration institution; international arbitration seated elsewhere is common in cross-border matters.

      Data protection

      Law No. 1.565 of 3 December 2024 on the protection of personal data · Regulator: Commission de Contrôle des Informations Nominatives (CCIN)

      Law No. 1.565 provides protection comparable to the GDPR, and Article 22 of the Constitution protects privacy and correspondence. For investigative work the key point is that processing for surveillance purposes, or concerning suspected offences, unlawful activity or security measures, requires prior authorisation from the CCIN. Transfers abroad are permitted to countries the CCIN considers equivalent; otherwise a CCIN transfer authorisation or a recognised exception, such as consent, is required, including where data is accessed from abroad. The CCIN has stated that employers may not access private messages in a work email account without the employee's presence and agreement.

      Private investigators

      We have not been able to verify from an official source the current statutory framework for private investigators (agents de recherches privées) in Monaco, including whether a specific authorisation is required and from which authority. Business activity in Monaco generally requires prior administrative authorisation, and the data protection law independently requires CCIN authorisation for surveillance-related processing, so engagements involving any work in the Principality are scoped with Monegasque counsel before they begin. Covert recording, physical surveillance and access to banking or administrative records are tightly constrained, and records are obtained only through lawful public access or judicial process.

      Corporate & public records

      Commercial companies and traders are registered in the Répertoire du Commerce et de l'Industrie (RCI), from which basic registration information can be obtained; the depth of information publicly available is narrower than in many EU registries. We have not confirmed from an official source the public access status of Monaco's beneficial ownership registers, and the FATF's published ratings indicate that transparency of legal arrangements such as trusts has been weaker than for companies. Many holding structures for Monaco residents sit in other jurisdictions. Property transactions are executed before Monegasque notaries, and court records are not systematically published online.

      Anti-corruption & AML

      Monaco's anti-money laundering framework is supervised by the Autorité Monégasque de Sécurité Financière (AMSF), which houses the financial intelligence unit and supervises reporting professionals. The FATF reports that Monaco's 2023 MONEYVAL mutual evaluation was followed by improvements in technical compliance recognised in December 2024. Bribery and corruption offences are set out in the Criminal Code. We have not verified the citation of the principal AML statute or recent anti-corruption amendments from a primary source.

      How TDSC Scopes Matters Touching Monaco

      TDSC scopes Monaco matters from Nashville, beginning with a jurisdictional review that addresses Law No. 1.565 and the CCIN authorisation requirement for surveillance-related processing, as well as banking and professional secrecy. Much relevant information sits outside Monaco, so research typically spans French, Italian, Swiss, British and offshore records alongside RCI information, published Monegasque judgments and open sources. Where any work is needed in the Principality itself, we coordinate appropriately authorised local professionals and work under the direction of Monegasque counsel, who also advise on judicial routes to information that is not public. We do not conduct surveillance in Monaco without confirmed legal basis, do not use pretexting and do not seek banking or administrative records outside lawful process.

      TDSC has no office in Monaco. Engagements are directed from Nashville, Tennessee; where work on the ground is needed, it is performed by appropriately licensed local professionals under a written scope. TDSC does not provide legal advice.

      Questions

      Monaco: Frequently Asked Questions

      Can surveillance be carried out in Monaco?

      Only with great care and a confirmed legal basis. Under Law No. 1.565, processing personal data for surveillance purposes or concerning suspected offences requires prior authorisation from the CCIN, in addition to any rules on the activity itself. We review any proposed observation with Monegasque counsel first, and in many matters lawful document and open-source research is the better route.

      How much company information is public in Monaco?

      Less than many clients expect. The RCI provides basic registration information, but ownership details, accounts and beneficial owners are not broadly published in the way they are in some EU states. Many structures used by Monaco residents sit in other jurisdictions, where we often find more useful records.

      Which courts hear commercial disputes in Monaco?

      The Tribunal de première instance hears commercial matters at first instance, with appeal to the Cour d'appel and final review on points of law by the Cour de révision. Proceedings are in French and follow procedure modelled on French law. We support Monegasque counsel with background research and evidence chronologies.

      Can personal data gathered in Monaco be sent to a US client?

      Transfers are permitted to countries the CCIN regards as providing equivalent protection; others require CCIN authorisation or an exception such as informed consent. This also applies when data held in Monaco is accessed from abroad. We plan collection so that transfers are lawful, or so that personal data is minimised from the outset.

      Who supervises anti-money laundering compliance in Monaco?

      The Autorité Monégasque de Sécurité Financière supervises reporting professionals and houses Monaco's financial intelligence unit. Following its 2023 MONEYVAL evaluation, Monaco's technical compliance was re-rated upward in 2024. Published AMSF sanctions decisions can be relevant in reputational research on financial intermediaries.
