      Overview

      Myanmar at a Glance

      Myanmar's business environment has been defined since the February 2021 coup by military rule, civil war and economic contraction. The state of emergency declared after the coup expired on 31 July 2025, and a phased general election held between December 2025 and January 2026 returned a large majority for the military-backed Union Solidarity and Development Party; voting did not take place in a number of conflict-affected constituencies. The courts, regulators and registries continue to operate in areas under central control, but independence and access are limited. Company records held in the Myanmar Companies Online (MyCO) register remain searchable, yet much of what matters to an investigation sits in informal networks, border economies and offshore holding structures.

      Organisations commission investigative work involving Myanmar mainly to manage exposure rather than to pursue opportunity. Apparel brands sourcing from Yangon-area factories, buyers of rubies, jade and other gemstones, traders in timber and minerals, and banks processing regional payments all need to know whether a counterparty is owned or controlled by the military, its conglomerates or designated persons. Asian investors exiting the market, insurers and creditors pursuing recoveries, and organisations responding to allegations of forced labour or human rights abuse in supply chains also commission research. Thailand, China and India are the principal economic neighbours, so work frequently extends across borders.

      Typical Matters

      Where Investigative Work Involving Myanmar Concentrates

      01

      Military ownership and control screening

      Determining whether a supplier, joint-venture partner or buyer is owned or controlled by the armed forces, military conglomerates or their associates, using MyCO extracts, leaked registry datasets, investigative journalism and civil-society research, and documenting the reasoning for compliance files.

      02

      Garment supply-chain due diligence

      Research on factory owners, intermediaries and subcontractors in the Yangon region for apparel brands and auditors, covering beneficial ownership, labour-rights allegations and links to sanctioned persons, typically combined with remote interviews conducted outside Myanmar.

      03

      Gemstones, jade and timber provenance

      Tracing ownership and trading chains for rubies, jade and teak that pass through Thai, Chinese and Indian markets, where provenance documentation may obscure Myanmar origin or connections to designated entities and conflict actors.

      04

      Exit and disposal counterparties

      Diligence on proposed purchasers and local partners when foreign investors divest Myanmar operations, focusing on whether the acquiring party is a front for designated persons or military-linked interests and on the reputational record of new owners.

      05

      Cross-border recovery research

      Supporting creditors and counsel by identifying assets and corporate interests of Myanmar-connected debtors held in Singapore, Thailand, Hong Kong and other regional centres, where enforcement prospects are generally stronger than in Myanmar itself.

      Legal Environment

      How the law shapes investigative work involving Myanmar

      Legal system

      Myanmar's legal system rests on common-law foundations inherited from the colonial period, with significant legal pluralism: Burmese customary law governs family matters for Buddhists, and customary justice operates alongside state courts. The court hierarchy runs from Township Courts through District Courts and the High Courts of the Regions and States to the Supreme Court of the Union. There are no specialised commercial courts. A statutory arbitration framework exists, but the independence and practical reliability of courts and enforcement since 2021 should be assessed matter by matter with counsel.

      Data protection

      No comprehensive data protection law; Law Protecting the Privacy and Security of Citizens (2017), Electronic Transactions Law (2004, amended 2021) and Cybersecurity Law (2025) contain relevant provisions · Regulator: No national data protection authority; the Information Technology and Cybersecurity Department receives cybersecurity incident reports

      Myanmar has no general data protection statute or dedicated regulator. Privacy and data rules are scattered across sectoral laws, and DLA Piper's guide records no enforcement as of February 2026. In practice the greater risk for investigative work is not regulatory sanction but exposure of sources and subjects: electronic communications and data held in-country may be accessible to the authorities. TDSC therefore applies the data protection standard of the client's own regime, minimises what is collected about individuals in Myanmar, and stores material outside the country.

      Private investigators

      We have not identified a licensing regime for private investigators in Myanmar, and we have not been able to verify the current rules on private investigative activity from official sources. Given the security environment, field enquiry, photography and interviews inside Myanmar can place local researchers and sources at serious personal risk, and activity that the authorities view as information-gathering may attract criminal exposure. Any proposal for in-country work is reviewed with counsel and assessed for the safety of everyone involved; in most matters TDSC relies on registry records, open sources and interviews conducted outside the country.

      Corporate & public records

      Companies are registered with the Directorate of Investment and Company Administration (DICA) under the Myanmar Companies Law 2017 through Myanmar Companies Online (MyCO), a fully electronic register that the public can search free of charge by company name or registration number, with official extracts available for purchase. Extracts typically assist in identifying directors and registered details, but nominee arrangements are common and declared ownership should not be taken at face value. Leaked DICA data published in 2021 has been widely used by researchers to map military-linked companies. Court and land records generally require local retrieval.

      Anti-corruption & AML

      The Anti-Corruption Law of September 2013 established the Anti-Corruption Commission of Myanmar, formed in February 2014, which investigates bribery and related offences involving public officials. Money laundering is addressed by dedicated anti-money laundering legislation, with a financial intelligence unit under the central authorities. The independence of enforcement bodies since 2021 is limited, and corruption allegations have been used against political opponents, so findings should be read in that context.

      Engagement Standard

      How TDSC Scopes Matters Touching Myanmar

      As a US company, TDSC screens every Myanmar-related matter for sanctions compliance before acceptance, including the list-based Burma program under Executive Order 14014 and other applicable regimes, and does not accept engagements that would violate US or other applicable sanctions. Most engagements are therefore lawful due diligence and risk advisory for clients outside Myanmar: identifying military or designated-person links, supporting divestment decisions and documenting supply-chain controls. Research relies on MyCO records, open sources, specialist civil-society datasets and interviews conducted outside the country. Any in-country activity is reviewed with counsel and with explicit attention to the safety of local professionals and sources. Material is held outside Myanmar.

      TDSC has no office in Myanmar. Engagements are directed from Nashville, Tennessee; where work on the ground is needed, it is performed by appropriately licensed local professionals under a written scope. TDSC does not provide legal advice.

      Questions

      Myanmar: Frequently Asked Questions

      Is doing business involving Myanmar prohibited for US persons?

      US sanctions on Burma are largely list-based rather than a full embargo: Executive Order 14014 of February 2021 authorises blocking of designated persons, including military-linked entities and officials. Transactions with designated parties, or entities they own, are prohibited. Whether a given transaction is permissible is a question for sanctions counsel; TDSC's role is to establish the facts.

      Can MyCO records show who really owns a Myanmar company?

      MyCO is publicly searchable and shows registered company details, and official extracts can be purchased. It records registered holders, not necessarily the people who control a business. Nominee shareholdings and family arrangements are common, so we compare registry data with leaked datasets, press and civil-society research and regional corporate filings before reaching a view.

      Do you send investigators into Myanmar?

      Rarely, and only after a specific assessment of legal and physical risk. Since 2021, information-gathering inside the country can endanger local researchers and the people they speak to. Most questions can be answered through registry work, open sources and interviews with people outside Myanmar, which is our default approach.

      How do you assess links to the military conglomerates?

      We look at shareholdings, directorships, shared addresses and personnel, procurement relationships and revenue flows, drawing on MyCO, leaked registry data, United Nations and civil-society reports and sanctions designations. Conclusions are graded by the strength of the evidence, and we distinguish documented ownership from reported association.

      What did the 2025-2026 election change for investigations?

      The phased election returned a large majority for the military-backed USDP, and the formal state of emergency had lapsed in July 2025. For investigative purposes, the practical constraints remain: armed conflict across many regions, limited judicial independence and heightened risk for local sources. Sanctions designations and counterparties should be rechecked at the time of each engagement.
