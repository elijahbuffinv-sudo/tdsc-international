      Overview

      Mauritius at a Glance

      Mauritius combines a French-derived civil law tradition with English-influenced company, evidence and procedural law, and it has built a substantial international financial services sector regulated by the Financial Services Commission. For investigators this means two distinct record environments: domestic companies registered with the Corporate and Business Registration Department, and licensed global business entities whose information also sits with the Financial Services Commission and with management companies. Public company searches confirm basic status, but director, shareholder and beneficial ownership details are more restricted than many clients expect. Data handling is governed by a GDPR-aligned statute that requires controllers and processors to register, which shapes how any engagement involving personal data is planned.

      Organisations commission work involving Mauritius because the island is widely used as a holding and investment jurisdiction for capital flowing into Africa and India, and because its funds, trusts and special-purpose companies frequently appear in transaction chains. Typical exposures include opaque ownership behind counterparties, asset tracing through layered Mauritian vehicles, disputes over fund management and shareholder rights, and reputational questions about promoters and directors. Tourism, real estate under foreign-investment schemes, and offshore business services also generate due diligence and dispute support needs. Many matters are commissioned by lenders, investors and law firms outside Mauritius who need to understand what a Mauritian entity is and who controls it.

      Jurisdictional Framework

      The rules that govern lawful inquiry in Mauritius

      Legal system

      Mauritius has a mixed legal system. Private law rests on French-inspired codes, including the Civil Code, Commercial Code and Code of Civil Procedure, while company and insolvency legislation, evidence and criminal procedure draw heavily on English law. The Supreme Court has unlimited jurisdiction and a Commercial Division for business disputes; intermediate and district courts hear smaller claims. Final appeals lie to the Judicial Committee of the Privy Council in London. Arbitration is supported by the Mauritius International Arbitration Centre (MIAC) and the Mediation and Arbitration Centre Mauritius (MARC).

      Data protection

      Data Protection Act 2017 (in force 15 January 2018) · Regulator: Data Protection Office (Data Protection Commissioner)

      The Data Protection Act 2017 replaced the 2004 Act and is closely aligned with the GDPR. Investigative work involving personal data needs a lawful basis, transparency unless an exemption applies, and data minimisation. Unusually, controllers and processors must register with the Data Protection Commissioner, with registration renewed every three years. Employee data is covered by the general rules, and special category data such as health information is restricted. Transfers of personal data out of Mauritius require appropriate safeguards evidenced to the Commissioner, explicit informed consent, or another listed ground such as necessity for legal claims, and the Commissioner may suspend or condition a transfer.

      Private investigators

      Private security service companies in Mauritius are licensed through the Commissioner of Police, with applications handled by the Private Security Section of the Mauritius Police Force. We have not been able to confirm from primary sources whether, or how, that regime applies to private investigation as distinct from guarding and security services, and we have not identified a separate licensing statute for private detectives. Engagements are therefore scoped with local counsel before any on-island enquiries begin. Covert recording, surveillance and handling of personal data are constrained by the Data Protection Act 2017 and the criminal law, and access to restricted records is available only through lawful channels.

      Corporate & public records

      The Registrar of Companies, within the Corporate and Business Registration Department (CBRD), maintains the company register. A free public search confirms name, legal form, status, registration date and identifiers; fuller director and shareholder information is generally released on written request to the Registrar and payment of a fee, or with consent. A central beneficial ownership register is held by the Registrar under the Companies Act 2001, but it is not public: access is limited to competent authorities, or by court order. Global business companies also file information with the Financial Services Commission. Court judgments of the Supreme Court are published, which supports litigation history research.

      Anti-corruption & AML

      The Financial Crimes Commission Act 2023 established the Financial Crimes Commission, which took over the functions of the Independent Commission Against Corruption, the Asset Recovery Investigation Division and the Integrity Reporting Services Agency, and repealed the Prevention of Corruption Act 2002 and the Asset Recovery Act 2011. The Financial Intelligence and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2002, as amended, continues to underpin the anti-money laundering framework, with the Financial Intelligence Unit and sector supervisors such as the Financial Services Commission and Bank of Mauritius.

      How TDSC Scopes Matters Touching Mauritius

      TDSC handles Mauritian matters from Nashville. Each engagement begins with a jurisdictional review: which entities are domestic companies and which are licensed global business entities, which records are public, which require a request to the Registrar or consent, and which are available only to authorities or by court order. We then work from lawful sources, including CBRD searches, Financial Services Commission information, published judgments, and open-source research in English and French. Where enquiries on the island are needed, we coordinate qualified local professionals and work alongside Mauritian counsel, particularly where disclosure applications are contemplated. Personal data is handled with the Data Protection Act 2017 registration and transfer rules in mind, and we do not access restricted registers or use pretexting.

      TDSC has no office in Mauritius. Engagements are directed from Nashville, Tennessee; where work on the ground is needed, it is performed by appropriately licensed local professionals under a written scope. TDSC does not provide legal advice.

      Questions

      Mauritius: Frequently Asked Questions

      Can you confirm the beneficial owner of a Mauritian company?

      Not from the central register itself. The beneficial ownership register held by the Registrar of Companies is not public and is available to competent authorities or by court order. We build an ownership picture from public CBRD information, filings obtainable through lawful requests, regulatory publications, judgments and open sources, and we state clearly which links are confirmed and which are inferred.

      Are global business companies harder to research than domestic companies?

      Often they are. Licensed global business entities file information with the Financial Services Commission and are typically administered by management companies, so much of their documentation sits outside public view. Useful material still exists in FSC publications, published decisions, court records and the public footprint of directors and management companies, which we review systematically.

      Does the Mauritian data protection law affect a US-based investigation?

      It can. The Data Protection Act 2017 applies to processing in Mauritius and restricts transfers of personal data out of the country unless safeguards or another legal ground apply. Where personal data will be gathered locally and sent abroad, we plan the engagement with Mauritian counsel and limit collection to what the matter actually requires.

      Which authority investigates corruption in Mauritius now?

      The Financial Crimes Commission, created under the Financial Crimes Commission Act 2023, took over the functions of the former Independent Commission Against Corruption and related asset recovery bodies. Older reports and judgments will refer to ICAC and the Prevention of Corruption Act 2002, so research covering earlier years needs to account for both regimes.

      Where are Mauritian commercial disputes heard?

      Larger commercial claims are heard by the Supreme Court, which has a Commercial Division, with appeals ultimately to the Judicial Committee of the Privy Council. Many cross-border disputes are arbitrated, with MIAC and MARC as the main local institutions. We support counsel with background research, chronology and asset information for both court and arbitral proceedings.
