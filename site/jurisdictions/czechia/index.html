      Overview

      Czechia at a Glance

      Czechia is an industrial EU economy with a transparent registry tradition. The business register (obchodní rejstřík), kept by the registry courts under the Public Registers Act, can be searched free of charge and includes the collection of documents where articles, financial statements and corporate resolutions are filed. Property is recorded in the cadastre, which can be consulted online. Personal data processing is governed by the GDPR and Act No. 110/2019 Coll., supervised by the Office for Personal Data Protection. Private detective services are a concession trade under the Trade Licensing Act, so on-the-ground enquiries require appropriately licensed local professionals.

      Organisations commission work involving Czechia because of its manufacturing depth and its role in German and wider EU supply chains. Automotive, engineering, electronics and energy companies, shared services centres in Prague and Brno, and a significant real estate and private equity market all generate investigative needs. Common exposures include supplier and distributor fraud, conflicts of interest in procurement, hidden ownership through holding structures in Cyprus, the Netherlands or Slovakia, misuse of EU subsidies, and asset recovery after insolvency. Integrity questions around public contracts and politically exposed persons also arise in due diligence on Czech counterparties.

      Law & Regulation

      What applies before any work touching Czechia begins

      Legal system

      Czechia has a civil law system. Private law was recodified by the Civil Code (Act No. 89/2012 Coll.) and the Business Corporations Act (Act No. 90/2012 Coll.), both in force since 2014. Commercial disputes are heard by the general courts: district and regional courts at first instance depending on the matter, high courts in Prague and Olomouc on appeal, and the Supreme Court in Brno on extraordinary appeal; the Constitutional Court protects fundamental rights. The best-known arbitral institution is the Arbitration Court attached to the Economic Chamber and the Agricultural Chamber of the Czech Republic in Prague.

      Data protection

      General Data Protection Regulation (EU) 2016/679 and Act No. 110/2019 Coll. on Personal Data Processing · Regulator: Office for Personal Data Protection (Úřad pro ochranu osobních údajů, ÚOOÚ)

      Act No. 110/2019 Coll., in force since 24 April 2019, implements the GDPR and replaced Act No. 101/2000 Coll. Investigations involving personal data need a GDPR lawful basis, usually legitimate interests or legal claims, supported by a documented balancing test and minimisation. Employee investigations must also respect the Labour Code's limits on monitoring employees and intrusions into their privacy. Birth numbers are not published in the business register and are not to be collected without a legal basis. Transfers of findings outside the EEA follow GDPR Chapter V through adequacy decisions, standard contractual clauses or a narrow derogation.

      Private investigators

      Private detective services (služby soukromých detektivů) are a concession trade under Act No. 455/1991 Coll., the Trade Licensing Act, which means a concession must be granted before the activity can be carried on. Requirements include professional qualifications combining education and practice, such as a university degree with a year of practice or a secondary school leaving certificate with three years of practice and relevant certification, together with reliability and a clean record for everyone who performs the work. Detectives have no special powers: they may not intercept communications, enter premises unlawfully or obtain restricted records, and their methods remain subject to the GDPR and criminal law.

      Corporate & public records

      The business register is kept by the registry courts under Act No. 304/2013 Coll. and can be searched online free of charge. It shows name, registered office, line of business, share capital, members of the statutory and supervisory bodies, authorised agents and insolvency or liquidation information; birth numbers are withheld. The collection of documents (sbírka listin) holds articles, financial statements and other filings, and certified copies can be obtained online free of charge. Beneficial owners are recorded in the beneficial ownership register (evidence skutečných majitelů), where public access has been narrowed since 2022. The cadastre of real estate can be consulted online.

      Anti-corruption & AML

      Bribery offences are set out in the Criminal Code (Act No. 40/2009 Coll.), and companies can be prosecuted under the Act on Criminal Liability of Legal Persons (Act No. 418/2011 Coll.), which allows a compliance defence. Anti-money laundering obligations derive from Act No. 253/2008 Coll., supervised by the Financial Analytical Office (FAÚ), the Czech financial intelligence unit. Corruption is investigated by the Police of the Czech Republic, notably the National Centre against Organised Crime, and prosecuted by the public prosecutor's offices.

      Typical Matters

      Where Investigative Work Involving Czechia Concentrates

      01

      Automotive supply chain integrity

      Investigation of supplier kickbacks, quality-related fraud and conflicts of interest in automotive and engineering supply chains in Bohemia and Moravia, where tiered suppliers, family-owned subcontractors and cross-border logistics create room for undisclosed related-party dealing.

      02

      Holding structure ownership

      Tracing ownership of Czech companies held through Cypriot, Dutch, Slovak or Luxembourg vehicles, combining the Czech business register and collection of documents with foreign registries to identify ultimate controllers in acquisitions and joint ventures.

      03

      EU subsidy and procurement risk

      Fact-finding into conflicts of interest and misuse of EU structural funds or public contracts, including links between beneficiaries, suppliers and politically exposed persons, prepared so that findings can be used by counsel or compliance teams.

      04

      Insolvency and asset recovery

      Identification of real estate, shareholdings and transfers before insolvency using the cadastre, the business register and the insolvency register, supporting creditors and administrators pursuing voidable transactions or enforcement of foreign judgments.

      05

      Shared services misconduct

      Internal investigations at Prague and Brno shared services and technology centres into expense fraud, data misuse or vendor collusion, scoped around the Labour Code's monitoring limits and GDPR requirements for employee data.

      How TDSC Scopes Matters Touching Czechia

      TDSC scopes Czech matters from Nashville with a review of the GDPR, Act No. 110/2019 Coll., Labour Code constraints where employees are involved, and the concession requirements for detective services. Desk research draws on the business register and collection of documents, the cadastre, the insolvency register, court decisions and Czech-language media.       Questions

      Czechia: Frequently Asked Questions

      Do private detectives need a licence in Czechia?

      Yes. Private detective services are a concession trade under the Trade Licensing Act, Act No. 455/1991 Coll. A concession requires qualifications combining education and practice, reliability and a clean record for those performing the work. TDSC is not licensed in Czechia and coordinates concession-holding local detectives when work in the country is needed.

      What can be learned from the Czech business register?

      A good deal. The register is free to search online and shows registered office, share capital, members of statutory and supervisory bodies, authorised agents and insolvency details. The collection of documents holds articles and financial statements, although filing compliance varies. Birth numbers of natural persons are not made public.

      Is the Czech beneficial ownership register public?

      Access has been narrowed. Following the Court of Justice of the EU's 2022 ruling on public access to beneficial ownership information, general public access was restricted. Obligated entities and persons who can demonstrate a legitimate interest may obtain information under the applicable rules, so ownership research also relies on the business register and foreign filings.

      Can Czech property ownership be verified remotely?

      Generally yes. The cadastre of real estate can be consulted online, showing parcels, buildings, owners and registered encumbrances, and certified extracts can be ordered. This allows asset searches and title checks to start without a site visit, with Czech counsel engaged for enforcement steps or disputed rights.

      Are companies criminally liable for bribery in Czechia?

      Yes. Act No. 418/2011 Coll. on the Criminal Liability of Legal Persons allows prosecution of companies for bribery and many other offences, with a defence available where the company made reasonable efforts to prevent the offence. This makes compliance programme evidence and third-party due diligence relevant in Czech investigations.
