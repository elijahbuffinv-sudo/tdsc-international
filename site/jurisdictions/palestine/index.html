      State of Palestine

      Palestine: Investigative Consulting

      Investigative consulting for matters touching Palestine means working across a fragmented legal and administrative map: Palestinian Authority law in parts of the West Bank, separate realities in Gaza and East Jerusalem, thin public records, and heightened sanctions and counter-terrorism screening for any counterparty.

      Overview

      Palestine at a Glance

      Palestine's legal environment is layered. Ottoman, British Mandate, Jordanian and Egyptian law, Israeli military orders and Palestinian Authority legislation all leave traces, and since the Legislative Council stopped sitting in 2007 most new law has been issued as presidential decree-laws. Commercial activity is concentrated in the West Bank, where the Ministry of National Economy registers companies under Decree-Law No. 42 of 2021. Gaza has been administered separately since 2007 and its institutions have been devastated by the conflict since October 2023, while East Jerusalem is administered under Israeli law. Any investigation therefore begins with a precise question: which authority, which records and which law govern the people, assets or events at issue.

      Organisations commission work touching Palestine for reasons that are often compliance-driven. International donors, NGOs and contractors delivering reconstruction and humanitarian programmes need partner and vendor vetting that addresses diversion, fraud and designated-party risk. Banks and payment providers dealing with Palestinian correspondents need source-of-funds and ownership clarity. Investors in telecommunications, banking, insurance, construction materials and agribusiness want background on family-held groups and their affiliations. Diaspora families and businesses in the Gulf, Jordan, the Americas and Europe also raise inheritance, property and partnership disputes in which documentary evidence is held locally and must be located and assessed with care.

      Typical Matters

      Where Investigative Work Involving Palestine Concentrates

      01

      Donor and NGO partner vetting

      Reconstruction and humanitarian funding brings partner, vendor and sub-grantee vetting: ownership and control, links to designated organisations, prior performance and integrity concerns. Work is calibrated to the donor's own sanctions and counter-terrorism obligations and documented so that decisions can be defended to auditors and funders.

      02

      Banking and correspondent due diligence

      Palestinian banks and money service businesses operate in a high-scrutiny environment. Enhanced due diligence on customers, correspondents and remitters focuses on ownership, source of wealth, adverse media in Arabic and English, and connections that would raise sanctions or diversion questions.

      03

      Family group and investor background

      Much of the private economy sits within family-held groups spanning the West Bank, Jordan and the Gulf. Investor and partner reviews map related companies, cross-border holdings and reputations, recognising that shareholder detail for private companies is rarely public.

      04

      Diaspora property and inheritance disputes

      Disputes over inherited land and family businesses frequently involve heirs abroad. Investigative support locates documents, identifies current occupants or managers and clarifies which authority's records apply, working through Palestinian counsel because land registration is incomplete and divided across administrative areas.

      05

      Procurement fraud and diversion

      Programmes delivering goods, construction and cash assistance face inflated pricing, conflicted suppliers and diversion. Engagements review procurement records, supplier relationships and payment flows, and support internal investigations for funders and implementing organisations.

      Jurisdictional Framework

      The rules that govern lawful inquiry in Palestine

      Legal system

      Palestine has a mainly civil law system with some common law influence, reflecting Ottoman, British Mandate, Jordanian (West Bank), Egyptian (Gaza) and Palestinian Authority sources. The regular courts are organised under the Law of the Formation of Regular Courts No. 5 of 2001: magistrate courts, courts of first instance, courts of appeal and a Court of Cassation, with no separate commercial court. Commercial disputes go to the regular civil courts. Arbitration is governed by Arbitration Law No. 3 of 2000, and arbitration is commonly used in commercial contracts.

      Data protection

      No comprehensive personal data protection law in force as of late 2025; a draft Personal Data Protection decree-law has been referred by the Cabinet to the President · Regulator: No dedicated data protection authority identified; the Ministry of Telecommunications and Digital Economy leads the draft law

      As of November 2025 the Cabinet had completed consultations on a Personal Data Protection Law and referred it to the President for approval; it should not be assumed to be in force without confirmation. Until it is, privacy rests on general constitutional and criminal provisions, banking secrecy, and the Cybercrime decree-law, which itself gives prosecutors broad powers over electronic data. For investigative work this means no single lawful-basis regime or transfer rule to apply, so TDSC applies its own data-minimisation standards, avoids collection that would breach confidentiality or cybercrime offences, and treats interview and employee data conservatively.

      Private investigators

      We have not identified a dedicated licensing regime for private investigators or detective agencies under Palestinian Authority law, and we have not located published guidance on their status. That is not the same as permission: general criminal provisions, the Cybercrime decree-law, banking secrecy and rules protecting private communications all constrain what can lawfully be gathered, and covert surveillance or recording carries clear legal and personal-safety risk. Investigative work touching Palestine is therefore scoped with Palestinian counsel, relies on open and lawfully obtainable sources, and treats any field enquiry as a matter for appropriately qualified local professionals.

      Corporate & public records

      Companies are registered with the Companies Registrar at the Ministry of National Economy under Decree-Law No. 42 of 2021, in force since April 2022. Extracts showing the company, its registered capital and principal officers can generally be obtained, but shareholder identities in private companies are not readily accessible in practice. Public shareholding companies listed on the Palestine Exchange disclose major holders and related-party dealings. There is no central beneficial ownership register; anti-corruption coalition AMAN notes the Companies Law does not require disclosure of beneficial owners. Land records are complicated by divided administration and incomplete registration.

      Anti-corruption & AML

      The Anti-Corruption Law issued in 2005 and amended since, including in 2018, is the basis for the Palestinian Anti-Corruption Commission (so named since 2010), which investigates corruption offences and refers cases for prosecution. Decree-Law No. 39 of 2022 on Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing is the current AML framework, following an earlier 2015 law. The Financial Follow-Up Unit acts as the financial intelligence unit, under the oversight of a national AML/CFT committee, and refers findings to the Public Prosecution.

      Engagement Standard

      How TDSC Scopes Matters Touching Palestine

      Engagements touching Palestine start with a jurisdictional map: whether the subject, assets or events sit in Palestinian Authority areas, Gaza, East Jerusalem or abroad, and which records and laws follow from that. Every matter is screened against US and other applicable sanctions and counter-terrorism designations before work begins, and TDSC declines work that would breach them. Research relies on open sources in Arabic and English, registry extracts obtainable through lawful channels and regional media. Where local enquiries are needed, TDSC works with Palestinian counsel and appropriately qualified local professionals, with safety assessed first. Personal data is minimised, held in Nashville under TDSC's own controls and shared only as the engagement requires.

      TDSC has no office in Palestine. Engagements are directed from Nashville, Tennessee; where work on the ground is needed, it is performed by appropriately licensed local professionals under a written scope. TDSC does not provide legal advice.

      Questions

      Palestine: Frequently Asked Questions

      Can company ownership in Palestine be verified from public records?

      Partly. The Companies Registrar at the Ministry of National Economy holds registration details, and listed companies on the Palestine Exchange disclose major shareholders. For private companies, shareholder identities are not readily accessible and there is no central beneficial ownership register, so ownership conclusions usually combine registry extracts, counsel's enquiries, corporate documents supplied by the counterparty and corroborating open sources.

      Does Palestine have a data protection law that applies to investigations?

      As of late 2025 a Personal Data Protection decree-law had been sent by the Cabinet to the President but we have not confirmed that it is in force. In the meantime there is no comprehensive regime or regulator. TDSC applies its own minimisation and security standards and respects banking secrecy, communications privacy and the Cybercrime decree-law, and will update scoping once the new law takes effect.

      How does the situation in Gaza affect investigative work?

      Since October 2023 records, institutions and infrastructure in Gaza have been severely disrupted, and field enquiries there are generally not feasible or safe. Work concerning Gaza-based parties relies on remote, open-source and documentary research, information held by counterparties and records outside Gaza, and its limitations are stated plainly in reporting.

      Why is sanctions screening emphasised for Palestinian counterparties?

      Certain organisations active in the territories are designated under US and other counter-terrorism regimes, and donors, banks and contractors face strict liability exposure for dealings that benefit them. Screening addresses direct designation, ownership and control, and associations reported in credible sources. It is a risk assessment, not a political judgement about individuals or communities.

      Can TDSC support a dispute over inherited family land?

      TDSC can help establish facts: locating records and documents, identifying heirs, occupants and intermediaries, and clarifying which authority's land records apply. Because land registration is incomplete and administration is divided, the legal strategy belongs to Palestinian counsel, and TDSC's work is designed to support their advice rather than replace it.
