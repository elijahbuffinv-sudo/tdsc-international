      Overview

      Eswatini at a Glance

      Eswatini is a landlocked kingdom bordered by South Africa and Mozambique, with a population of about 1.26 million. Its legal order combines Roman-Dutch common law, statute and Swazi law and custom under the 2005 Constitution. Commercial and corporate law draw heavily on South African models, and many professionals in Mbabane and Manzini trained or practise across the border. Several reforms are still in progress: a new Companies Act and an online companies registry have been proposed by the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Trade, and the Data Protection Act of 2022 is still being implemented. In practice, much investigative fact-finding relies on records held at registries and court offices, and on careful, discreet enquiries.

      Organisations commission work involving Eswatini for several reasons. The country is a member of the Southern African Customs Union and the Common Monetary Area, and its currency, the lilangeni, is pegged to the South African rand. Sugar, wood pulp, soft-drink concentrate and textiles link it to regional and international supply chains. Investors, lenders and buyers want to know who stands behind local counterparties, how land and concessions are held, and whether a partner has links to politically exposed persons in a system where economic and political influence overlap. Others need help tracing assets that have moved between Eswatini and South Africa, or checking claims made in a commercial dispute.

      Typical Matters

      Where Investigative Work Involving Eswatini Concentrates

      01

      Counterparty and ownership checks

      Establishing who controls an Eswatini company, distributor or landholding partner, using registry filings, court records and lawful local enquiries. This is especially important where no public beneficial ownership register exists and nominee arrangements or family holdings can obscure the people who actually make decisions.

      02

      Politically exposed person exposure

      In Eswatini, state-linked investment vehicles, traditional authorities and private business are closely connected. Clients entering joint ventures, concessions or supply agreements often need a documented view of political connections, reputational history and potential conflicts before committing capital or signing long-term contracts.

      03

      Cross-border asset tracing with South Africa

      Money, vehicles and property move easily across the South African border within the Common Monetary Area. Asset-tracing work for judgment creditors and insolvency practitioners often has to combine Eswatini deeds and court records with South African sources, coordinated with counsel in both countries.

      04

      Agricultural and manufacturing supply chains

      Sugar, forestry and textile exports place Eswatini suppliers within international procurement and responsible-sourcing programmes. Buyers commission checks on supplier ownership, labour-related litigation and public allegations to support supplier onboarding, audits and responses to customer or NGO enquiries.

      05

      Fraud and misconduct inquiries

      Multinationals and development-funded projects sometimes need fact-finding into procurement irregularities, conflicts of interest or misappropriation involving Eswatini staff or vendors. This work has to respect the Data Protection Act of 2022, employment law and the role of the Anti-Corruption Commission.

      Legal Environment

      How the law shapes investigative work involving Eswatini

      Legal system

      Eswatini has a mixed legal system. Roman-Dutch common law and statute operate alongside uncodified Swazi law and custom, and the 2005 Constitution is the supreme law. The Supreme Court is the final court of appeal. The High Court has unlimited original civil and criminal jurisdiction and hears commercial disputes. Magistrates' Courts handle lower-value matters. The Industrial Court hears employment matters, and Swazi National Courts apply customary law. We have not identified a specialised commercial court or a dedicated international commercial arbitration centre. Commercial parties often choose arbitration seated in South Africa.

      Data protection

      Data Protection Act, 2022 (Act No. 5 of 2022) · Regulator: Eswatini Communications Commission (ESCCOM)

      The Data Protection Act of 2022 gives Eswatini a comprehensive personal data framework, with the Eswatini Communications Commission as the supervisory authority. Controllers must process personal data lawfully and transparently and for specified purposes. Sensitive categories, such as health, race, religion and political beliefs, may only be processed where the Act permits. Transfers to other SADC member states are allowed under conditions, and transfers elsewhere require adequate protection. ESCCOM has consulted on the registration of data controllers and processors. Investigative work involving Eswatini residents or employees therefore needs a documented purpose, data minimisation and careful handling of any cross-border transfer to the United States.

      Private investigators

      We have not identified a dedicated statute that licenses private investigators or detective agencies in Eswatini, and we could not confirm which authority, if any, oversees investigative firms as distinct from security guarding companies. TDSC therefore treats the position as uncertain. Before any local enquiries, engagements are scoped with Eswatini counsel. General law still applies: the criminal law, constitutional privacy protections, the Data Protection Act of 2022 and rules on trespass and the interception of communications. Covert recording, surveillance of individuals and any attempt to obtain police, telecommunications or banking records are outside what TDSC will do or commission.

      Corporate & public records

      Companies are incorporated under the Companies Act of 2009 and filings are lodged with the Registrar of Companies under the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Trade. A replacement Companies Act and an online registry have been proposed but, as of 2025, we could not confirm that either is fully operational. Searches of registered details, directors and filed returns generally require an in-person or agent-assisted request rather than an online lookup. We have not identified a publicly accessible beneficial ownership register. Title deeds are held by the Deeds Registry, and High Court judgments are published through EswatiniLII.

      Anti-corruption & AML

      The Prevention of Corruption Act of 2006 establishes the Anti-Corruption Commission. It criminalises bribery of public officers, corrupt dealings in contracts and tenders, and corruption in private transactions. It also presumes corruption where a public officer's property or lifestyle cannot be explained by their official income. The Money Laundering and Financing of Terrorism (Prevention) Act of 2011 sets customer due diligence and reporting duties for accountable institutions and establishes the Eswatini Financial Intelligence Unit, which is still referred to in the statute as the Swaziland Financial Intelligence Unit.

      TDSC handles matters touching Eswatini from Nashville as scoped consulting engagements.       Questions

      Eswatini: Frequently Asked Questions

      Can we find out who ultimately owns a company registered in Eswatini?

      Often in part. Registry filings may show directors and shareholders, but we have not identified a public beneficial ownership register, and nominee or family arrangements are common. TDSC combines registry and court records with lawful open-source research and, where appropriate, discreet enquiries through local counsel, then reports clearly which conclusions are documented and which are inferred.

      Are company records in Eswatini searchable online?

      As of 2025, we could not confirm a fully operational online companies registry. The government has proposed a new Companies Act and online registry. Until that is in place, searches usually require a request at the Registrar of Companies in Mbabane, made by a local attorney or agent. Turnaround times can vary.

      Does the Data Protection Act of 2022 affect background research on Eswatini individuals?

      Yes. The Act regulates the collection and use of personal data and is supervised by the Eswatini Communications Commission. It restricts the processing of sensitive categories and sets conditions for transfers outside Eswatini. TDSC documents the purpose of each inquiry, limits collection to what is relevant and handles transfers to the United States with appropriate safeguards.

      How do South African connections affect an Eswatini investigation?

      Significantly. Eswatini shares a monetary union and long trade ties with South Africa, and many businesspeople hold assets, companies or bank relationships across the border. Asset tracing and ownership research often have to cover South African company, deeds and court records as well. TDSC scopes this with counsel in each jurisdiction.

      Who investigates corruption in Eswatini?

      The Anti-Corruption Commission, established under the Prevention of Corruption Act of 2006, investigates corruption offences, and the Eswatini Financial Intelligence Unit receives suspicious transaction reports under the 2011 anti-money laundering Act. TDSC's private fact-finding does not replace these bodies. Where findings suggest a possible offence, clients should take advice from local counsel on reporting.
