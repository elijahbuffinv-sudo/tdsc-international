      Overview

      Lesotho at a Glance

      Lesotho is a constitutional monarchy entirely surrounded by South Africa, with a legal system that combines Roman-Dutch civil law, English common law and Basotho customary law codified in the Laws of Lerotholi. The 1993 Constitution is supreme, and a specialised Commercial Division of the High Court exists to hear commercial disputes. For investigators, the environment is small and personal: public records are limited compared with larger jurisdictions, much commercial activity links directly to South African companies and banks, and customary land and family arrangements can matter as much as formal registrations. A dedicated data protection statute and an independent anti-corruption directorate set the boundaries for lawful work.

      Organisations commission investigative work involving Lesotho for a handful of recurring reasons. The textile and apparel sector supplies international brands and attracts supply-chain and labour-standards scrutiny. Diamond mining in the highlands and the Lesotho Highlands Water Project involve large contracts, foreign investors and public procurement, which bring corruption and integrity questions. Development finance institutions and donors fund infrastructure and need vetting of contractors and intermediaries. Cross-border matters with South Africa, including asset tracing, debt recovery and verification of directors, are also common, and litigation counsel may need background on Basotho parties to commercial or employment disputes.

      Jurisdictional Framework

      The rules that govern lawful inquiry in Lesotho

      Legal system

      Lesotho has a mixed legal system of Roman-Dutch civil law and English common law, applied alongside Basotho customary law. The Constitution of 1993 is supreme. The Court of Appeal is the final appellate court and the High Court has unlimited original jurisdiction in civil and criminal matters. Commercial disputes can be heard in a specialised commercial court established to accelerate commercial cases, while the Labour Court hears employment matters and Local and Central Courts apply customary law. Magistrates' Courts hear lower-value claims. Lesotho follows a dualist approach to treaties.

      Data protection

      Data Protection Act, 2013 · Regulator: Data Protection Commission, established under Part 2 of the Act

      The Data Protection Act 2013 requires a lawful basis for processing personal information, such as consent, contract, legal obligation or legitimate interests, and generally expects information to be collected directly from the individual, with exceptions including public records. Genetic, biometric, health, children's and criminal-offence data are restricted unless an exemption applies. Controllers must notify the Data Protection Commission before processing. Transfers to SADC states that have adopted SADC data protection requirements are subject to necessity tests, and transfers elsewhere require an adequate level of protection. Background research on individuals therefore needs a documented purpose and careful source selection.

      Private investigators

      We have not identified a dedicated licensing regime for private investigators in Lesotho in the sources available to us, and we have not confirmed whether private security legislation extends to investigative services. Engagements are therefore scoped with Lesotho counsel before any local inquiries are made. Restrictions apply regardless: the Data Protection Act 2013 governs collection and use of personal information, the Constitution recognises a right to privacy, and the Internal Security Act restricts disclosure of certain information. TDSC does not undertake interception, covert recording or pretexting, and any field inquiries are limited to lawful, documented methods confirmed in advance.

      Corporate & public records

      Companies are registered with the Registrar of Companies under the Ministry of Trade, and incorporation is handled through the One-Stop Business Facilitation Centre in Maseru. We have not confirmed the scope of public online search access or the extent of director and shareholder information available to third parties, so registry research is arranged through local counsel or agents. Lesotho committed in July 2020 to beneficial ownership transparency and is listed by Open Ownership as implementing a register; public access has not been confirmed. Land records are kept under the Lands Registry Act 1967, and customary land allocation can complicate ownership questions.

      Anti-corruption & AML

      The Prevention of Corruption and Economic Offences Act No. 5 of 1999, amended by Act No. 8 of 2006, establishes the Directorate on Corruption and Economic Offences (DCEO), which investigates corruption and also serves as the anti-money laundering authority. The Money Laundering and Proceeds of Crime Act No. 4 of 2008 criminalises money laundering and establishes the Financial Intelligence Unit, which receives and analyses financial information for law enforcement. Lesotho is a member of ESAAMLG and was evaluated in 2023.

      01

      Apparel supply-chain due diligence

      Reviewing ownership, management and reputational history of garment and textile manufacturers in Maseru and Maputsoe that supply international brands, including links to foreign-owned parent groups, labour-standards controversies and changes of control, to support sourcing and responsible business decisions.

      02

      Mining and water project integrity

      Background research on contractors, subcontractors and local partners connected to highland diamond mining and the Lesotho Highlands Water Project, focusing on public-record corruption allegations, DCEO matters, related-party links and politically exposed persons involved in procurement or licensing.

      03

      Cross-border South Africa asset tracing

      Following assets and business interests of Basotho debtors and directors into South Africa, where most banking, property and trading relationships sit, by combining South African company and deeds records with Lesotho court and open-source research for counsel pursuing recovery.

      04

      Development-funded contractor vetting

      Supporting donors, development finance institutions and implementing agencies with integrity checks on suppliers, consultants and grantees in Lesotho, including verification of claimed experience, registration status and prior debarment or adverse findings, documented to meet funder procurement standards.

      05

      Litigation and employment dispute support

      Assembling lawful background on parties to commercial, employment or shareholder disputes before the High Court, Commercial Division or Labour Court, including prior litigation, business affiliations and public statements, for use by Lesotho or South African counsel.

      Engagement Standard

      How TDSC Scopes Matters Touching Lesotho

      TDSC starts each Lesotho matter by mapping where the relevant records and assets are likely to sit, which is often partly in South Africa. Research draws on court records, official notices, procurement publications, media and South African registries, and on Lesotho registry extracts obtained through local counsel or agents. Because we have not confirmed a licensing regime for investigators, any local inquiries are agreed in writing with Lesotho counsel and carried out only by qualified local professionals using lawful methods. Personal information is handled in line with the Data Protection Act 2013, limited to the stated purpose and stored in the United States. TDSC does not offer legal advice; findings are reported to the client and its counsel for their assessment.

      TDSC has no office in Lesotho. Engagements are directed from Nashville, Tennessee; where work on the ground is needed, it is performed by appropriately licensed local professionals under a written scope. TDSC does not provide legal advice.

      Questions

      Lesotho: Frequently Asked Questions

      Can you identify the owners of a Lesotho company?

      Usually, but expectations should be realistic. Registry extracts are obtained through local counsel or agents, and the scope of publicly available shareholder and director data has not been confirmed for every company type. A beneficial ownership register is being implemented following a 2020 commitment. TDSC supplements registry data with South African records, court filings and open sources.

      Is our matter likely to involve South Africa as well?

      Often. Lesotho's currency is pegged to the rand, much of its banking and trade runs through South Africa, and many Basotho businesses have South African directors, suppliers or property. TDSC routinely scopes Lesotho matters to include South African company, deeds and court records where the facts point there.

      How does Lesotho's data protection law affect background research?

      The Data Protection Act 2013 requires a lawful basis, limits processing of sensitive categories such as criminal-offence and health data, and expects collection directly from the individual unless an exception such as public records applies. TDSC documents the purpose of each inquiry, relies on public and lawful sources, and limits what is collected and retained.

      Which authority handles corruption complaints in Lesotho?

      The Directorate on Corruption and Economic Offences, established under the 1999 Act, investigates corruption and economic offences and also acts as the anti-money laundering authority. TDSC does not file complaints for clients, but its reports can help counsel decide whether a referral to the DCEO or other authorities is appropriate.

      Can TDSC investigate customary land interests?

      TDSC can research formal land records held under the Lands Registry Act 1967 through local counsel and identify related court matters and public information. Customary allocation and family arrangements may not be fully reflected in formal records, so conclusions about land interests are framed carefully and reviewed by Lesotho counsel.
