      Overview

      Greece at a Glance

      Greece is an EU member state with a civil-law system, a court structure divided between civil, criminal and administrative jurisdictions, and a corporate registry, the General Commercial Register (GEMI), that publishes company data online. Personal data is protected under the GDPR, Law 4624/2019 and an active Hellenic Data Protection Authority, and Greek law attaches criminal penalties to unlawful processing of personal data in serious cases. Investigative work therefore depends on public records, published court and regulatory material and Greek-language media, with any inquiry into individuals scoped carefully against a documented lawful basis. Court proceedings can be lengthy, which makes thorough pre-transaction and pre-litigation research particularly valuable.

      Organisations commission work touching Greece because of its global role in ship-owning and ship finance, a privatisation and infrastructure programme that has drawn foreign investors, a real estate market attractive to international buyers, and tourism and energy projects. Typical exposures include verifying the beneficial owners behind ship-owning companies registered abroad but managed from Piraeus, reviewing counterparties in public procurement and EU-funded projects, assessing partners in hospitality and renewable energy developments, and tracing assets for creditors after commercial failures. Lenders and insurers also seek background research on Greek principals whose wealth and business interests are held through family structures in several jurisdictions.

      Jurisdictional Framework

      The rules that govern lawful inquiry in Greece

      Legal system

      Greece has a civil-law system based on the 1975 Constitution and codified law, principally the Civil Code, the Code of Civil Procedure and the Penal Code. Civil and commercial disputes are heard at first instance by Magistrates' Courts or Courts of First Instance depending on value, with appeals to Courts of First Instance or Courts of Appeal and final review by the Court of Cassation (Areios Pagos). Administrative disputes go to separate administrative courts under the Council of State. All courts may review whether legislation conforms to the Constitution.

      Data protection

      General Data Protection Regulation (EU) 2016/679 and Law 4624/2019 · Regulator: Hellenic Data Protection Authority (HDPA)

      Investigative processing of personal data needs a lawful basis under the GDPR, typically legitimate interests or the establishment and defence of legal claims, supported by a documented balancing test. Law 4624/2019 supplements the GDPR and provides criminal penalties for unauthorised processing, with heavier penalties where sensitive data is involved or the aim is unlawful gain or harm. Employee data may be processed for recruitment and performance of the employment contract; consent is only an exceptional basis. Workplace CCTV is limited to safety and security and may not be used to evaluate staff. Transfers outside the EEA require a GDPR transfer mechanism.

      Private investigators

      We have not verified, from sources reviewed for this page, the current statutory position on private investigators in Greece. Private security services are regulated separately, and the status of investigative activity by private firms should be confirmed with Greek counsel for each engagement. Whatever the licensing position, Greek law gives private parties no special investigative powers: access to tax, banking, telecommunications and police records is restricted, unlawful processing of personal data can be a criminal offence under Law 4624/2019, and covert recording or surveillance of individuals carries significant legal risk. TDSC scopes all field activity in Greece with local counsel before it begins.

      Corporate & public records

      The General Commercial Register (GEMI), accessible through the Business Portal, records Greek companies and publishes company data and filings, including registration details, management, articles and published financial statements, with a document authenticity service. Greece maintains a central register of beneficial owners through the government's digital services platform; following the November 2022 ruling of the Court of Justice of the European Union, access is moving to a legitimate-interest basis under EU rules due to apply from 2026. Land rights are recorded in the Hellenic Cadastre, which is progressively replacing the older mortgage registry system.

      Anti-corruption & AML

      Active and passive bribery of public officials are offences under Articles 235 and 236 of the Penal Code, which extend to EU officials. Law 5090/2024 introduced liability for legal entities for corruption offences. Money laundering is addressed by Law 4557/2018, as amended. The Prosecutor's Office against Financial and Economic Crime leads corruption investigations, supported by the Special Body of Investigators and the Financial Police, while the National Transparency Authority coordinates prevention. The European Public Prosecutor's Office handles offences against EU financial interests.

      Engagement Standard

      How TDSC Scopes Matters Touching Greece

      TDSC begins every Greek matter with a jurisdictional review covering the purpose of the inquiry, the GDPR lawful basis for any processing of personal data, the criminal exposure that unlawful processing can carry under Law 4624/2019, and how findings may be used in Greek or foreign proceedings. Research draws on GEMI filings, published court decisions, the Government Gazette, shipping and maritime databases and Greek-language press. Where local work is needed, such as document retrieval or discreet inquiries, TDSC coordinates appropriately authorised Greek professionals and works with the client's Greek counsel, who advises on legal questions and on any licensing requirement. Personal data is minimised, transferred under an appropriate GDPR mechanism and deleted at the end of the engagement.

      TDSC has no office in Greece. Engagements are directed from Nashville, Tennessee; where work on the ground is needed, it is performed by appropriately licensed local professionals under a written scope. TDSC does not provide legal advice.

      Questions

      Greece: Frequently Asked Questions

      Can the beneficial owners of a Greek ship-owning group be identified?

      Often, through lawful sources. Many vessels are owned by single-ship companies registered outside Greece and managed from Piraeus, so research combines maritime databases, GEMI records of the Greek management company, published financing and court material, and press. Results are presented with the confidence level each source supports.

      Is Greek corporate information available online?

      Yes, to a significant extent. The General Commercial Register (GEMI) publishes company registration data and filings, including published financial statements, through the Business Portal. The beneficial ownership register is not open to the general public; access under EU rules depends on a legitimate interest, so it is approached through proper channels.

      Are there criminal risks in collecting personal data in Greece?

      Yes. Law 4624/2019 provides criminal penalties for unauthorised processing of personal data, with heavier penalties where sensitive data is involved or the purpose is unlawful gain or harm. This makes a documented lawful basis, data minimisation and counsel review essential before any inquiry about individuals begins.

      How long do commercial disputes take in Greek courts?

      Proceedings can be lengthy, particularly where appeals reach the Court of Cassation. For that reason clients often commission research before filing, to assess the counterparty's assets and the realistic prospect of recovery, and coordinate with Greek counsel on interim measures where assets may be moved.

      What should be checked before buying property in Greece?

      Beyond legal title, which is a matter for Greek counsel and notaries, clients commonly verify the seller's identity and corporate standing, related litigation, the developer's track record and any reputational issues. Cadastre coverage has expanded, but older records and inheritance-related claims can still raise questions.
