      New Zealand: Investigative Consulting

      Investigative consulting for matters touching New Zealand works with unusually open company records, a principles-based Privacy Act that now regulates indirect collection, and a licensing regime under which anyone carrying on business as a private investigator must hold a licence from the Private Security Personnel Licensing Authority.

      Overview

      New Zealand at a Glance

      New Zealand is a small, transparent and well-administered economy, and that shapes investigative work. The Companies Office maintains the Companies Register, the Financial Service Providers Register and the Personal Property Securities Register, all searchable online, so documentary research can go further than in many larger jurisdictions. Personal information is governed by the Privacy Act 2020 and its information privacy principles, overseen by the Privacy Commissioner; the Privacy Amendment Act 2025 adds IPP 3A on indirect collection, in effect since 1 May 2026. Private investigation is a licensed occupation under the Private Security Personnel and Private Investigators Act 2010. The Serious Fraud Office leads on serious or complex fraud, bribery and corruption.

      Organisations commission work involving New Zealand for a mix of trade, investment and dispute reasons. Agriculture, dairy, forestry, tourism, technology and infrastructure attract overseas capital and partners, and Australian and Asian groups frequently hold New Zealand subsidiaries. Typical exposures include counterparties whose public profile overstates their track record, director histories involving failed companies, assets secured or shifted through personal property security interests, and contractors or advisers in public infrastructure procurement. Insurers, employers and law firms outside New Zealand also need background, asset and witness work that respects the Privacy Act and the limits on what a licensed investigator may do.

      Typical Matters

      Where Investigative Work Involving New Zealand Concentrates

      01

      Director and counterparty histories

      Background reviews of New Zealand directors and shareholders using the Companies Register, including prior directorships of liquidated companies, director prohibition notices and court judgments, to test whether a proposed partner's track record matches the account presented to investors or lenders.

      02

      Asset tracing and PPSR analysis

      Identification of companies, shareholdings and secured interests connected to a debtor or respondent, using Companies Office and Personal Property Securities Register records, so that counsel can assess enforcement options for a foreign judgment or arbitral award in New Zealand.

      03

      Agribusiness and land-based investment

      Due diligence on dairy, horticulture, forestry and other land-based ventures and their operators, covering ownership chains, regulatory history and disputes, for overseas investors and lenders assessing New Zealand assets, operators or prospective joint-venture partners before committing capital.

      04

      Insurance and claims enquiries

      Support to insurers and their counsel on questionable claims involving New Zealand claimants or assets, with any field observation or interviews coordinated through PSPLA-licensed investigators and scoped against the Privacy Act and the new IPP 3A notification duty.

      05

      Financial services provider checks

      Verification of registration, ownership and history of firms on the Financial Service Providers Register, a recurring issue where entities use New Zealand registration to present credibility to clients and counterparties located elsewhere, often with little genuine local presence.

      Legal Environment

      How the law shapes investigative work involving New Zealand

      Legal system

      New Zealand has a common law system derived from English law, with no single codified constitution; key statutes, the Treaty of Waitangi and judicial decisions together form its constitutional framework. Commercial disputes are heard in the District Court or the High Court depending on value and complexity, with appeals to the Court of Appeal and, with leave, the Supreme Court. Arbitration is governed by the Arbitration Act 1996, whose Schedule 1 corresponds for the most part to the UNCITRAL Model Law on International Commercial Arbitration, adapted for New Zealand.

      Data protection

      Privacy Act 2020, as amended by the Privacy Amendment Act 2025 · Regulator: Office of the Privacy Commissioner

      The Privacy Act 2020 applies principles rather than consent-based lawful bases: collection must be for a lawful purpose connected to the agency's functions, and since 1 May 2026 IPP 3A has required agencies that collect personal information indirectly to make individuals aware of the collection, subject to exceptions, which bears directly on investigative research. Cross-border disclosure under IPP 12 requires authorisation, comparable safeguards such as contractual clauses, or a comparable legal regime. Notifiable privacy breaches likely to cause serious harm must be reported to the Commissioner and affected individuals as soon as practicable. A Biometric Processing Privacy Code has applied since November 2025.

      Private investigators

      Private investigation is licensed under the Private Security Personnel and Private Investigators Act 2010, in force since 1 April 2011. A private investigator is someone who, for valuable consideration, carries on a business seeking or supplying information about a person's character, actions, financial position, occupation, identity or whereabouts, excluding information in a public record. Such a person must hold a licence, and individual employees need a certificate of approval, both issued by the Private Security Personnel Licensing Authority, which keeps a public register. Licensees must state in correspondence that they have no more authority than an ordinary private citizen to require a reply.

      Corporate & public records

      The Companies Office maintains the Companies Register, which can be searched by company, director or shareholder name, along with the Financial Service Providers Register, the Disclose Register and the Personal Property Securities Register, which records security interests over personal property and is useful in asset work. It also publishes notices such as director prohibitions. Since a 2025 law change, eligible directors may substitute an alternative address for their home address on the register. The Companies Office page reviewed does not describe a register of beneficial owners, so ownership above the registered shareholder level generally requires further research.

      Anti-corruption & AML

      Bribery of officials, including provisions relating to foreign public officials, sits in Part 6 of the Crimes Act 1961, while the Secret Commissions Act 1910 criminalises corrupt payments to agents in the private sector, with prosecutions requiring the leave of the Attorney-General. The Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009 imposes customer due diligence and reporting obligations on reporting entities. The Serious Fraud Office leads investigation and prosecution of serious or complex fraud, bribery and corruption, alongside New Zealand Police.

      Engagement Standard

      How TDSC Scopes Matters Touching New Zealand

      TDSC scopes New Zealand matters from Nashville by settling the purpose of the enquiry first, because under the Privacy Act 2020 that purpose determines what may be collected and, since May 2026, how IPP 3A affects indirect collection. Documentary research relies on the Companies Office registers, the Personal Property Securities Register, published judgments and media. Where an enquiry would amount to carrying on business as a private investigator in New Zealand, such as interviews, field observation or locating individuals, TDSC coordinates a PSPLA-licensed local investigator and, where proceedings are contemplated, New Zealand counsel. Disclosures of findings outside New Zealand are planned with IPP 12 in mind, and case files are held with restricted access and defined retention.

      TDSC has no office in New Zealand. Engagements are directed from Nashville, Tennessee; where work on the ground is needed, it is performed by appropriately licensed local professionals under a written scope. TDSC does not provide legal advice.

      Questions

      New Zealand: Frequently Asked Questions

      Is a licence required to work as a private investigator in New Zealand?

      Yes. Under the Private Security Personnel and Private Investigators Act 2010, anyone carrying on business as a private investigator for valuable consideration must hold a licence, and employees need a certificate of approval, from the Private Security Personnel Licensing Authority. Breach is an offence. TDSC is not licensed in New Zealand and coordinates licensed investigators for local work.

      What does IPP 3A change for background research?

      The Privacy Amendment Act 2025 adds information privacy principle 3A, which since 1 May 2026 has required agencies collecting personal information about someone from another source to take steps to make that person aware of the collection, subject to exceptions. Investigative scoping now needs to consider whether an exception applies before indirect research begins.

      Are shareholders and directors of New Zealand companies public?

      The Companies Register can be searched by company, director or shareholder name, which makes it one of the more open company registers internationally. Eligible directors may now substitute an alternative address for their home address. The register reviewed does not include a separate beneficial ownership register, so nominee and trust structures still require further work.

      Can the Personal Property Securities Register help locate assets?

      Often. The register records security interests over personal property such as vehicles, equipment, inventory and receivables, and identifies secured parties. A search can show what a debtor has pledged and to whom, which helps counsel judge recoverability and identify other creditors before enforcing a judgment or award in New Zealand.

      Who investigates bribery and corporate fraud in New Zealand?

      The Serious Fraud Office is the lead agency for serious or complex fraud, bribery and corruption, alongside New Zealand Police. Relevant offences appear in the Crimes Act 1961 and the Secret Commissions Act 1910. TDSC can assemble documentary findings for counsel considering a referral but does not give legal advice on reporting decisions.
