      Investigations Touching Belgium

      Investigative consulting for matters touching Belgium means working within a new licensing regime for private investigations, GDPR enforced by an active national authority, and a federal state whose records, courts and languages divide along regional and community lines.

      Overview

      Belgium at a Glance

      Belgium is a federal state with three regions and three language communities, and that structure shapes investigative work. Court proceedings run in Dutch, French or German depending on the district, and filings in Brussels may be in either Dutch or French. Company identity data is centralised in the Crossroads Bank for Enterprises, but shareholder details, articles of association and annual accounts sit in separate sources. Since December 2024 private investigations are governed by a new statute that tightens licensing, reporting and the rights of persons interviewed. Evidence gathered in breach of certain of its provisions can be excluded by a judge, so method matters as much as result.

      Brussels hosts the principal EU institutions, NATO and many international associations, and Antwerp is one of Europe's largest ports and a long-standing centre of the diamond trade. Chemicals, pharmaceuticals, logistics and financial services are significant sectors, and many multinational groups run European holding or treasury entities from Belgium. Organisations commission investigative work involving Belgium for acquisition and counterparty due diligence, supply-chain and port-related fraud, procurement and lobbying integrity questions, misconduct allegations within European operations, and the tracing of assets held through Belgian companies or real estate in support of litigation or enforcement elsewhere.

      Jurisdictional Framework

      The rules that govern lawful inquiry in Belgium

      Legal system

      Belgium has a civil law system rooted in the Napoleonic codes, now being replaced book by book by a new Civil Code, alongside the Code of Companies and Associations (2019) and the Code of Economic Law. Commercial disputes between enterprises are heard by the enterprise courts (tribunal de l'entreprise / ondernemingsrechtbank), renamed from commercial courts in the 2018 business law reform, with appeals to the courts of appeal and the Court of Cassation. CEPANI, the Belgian Centre for Arbitration and Mediation in Brussels, is the main domestic arbitral institution.

      Data protection

      General Data Protection Regulation (EU) 2016/679; Data Protection Act of 30 July 2018; Act of 3 December 2017 establishing the Data Protection Authority · Regulator: Data Protection Authority (Gegevensbeschermingsautoriteit / Autorité de protection des données)

      Investigative work involving people in Belgium is subject to the GDPR as supplemented by the Data Protection Act of 30 July 2018. Each processing operation needs a lawful basis, usually legitimate interests documented through a balancing test. Data about criminal offences may only be processed on one of the legal bases listed in the Act, with access lists and confidentiality duties. Employee investigations must also respect Belgian labour law and, since December 2024, the procedural safeguards of the Private Investigations Act. Transfers of personal data to the United States or elsewhere outside the EEA follow the GDPR transfer rules; the Data Protection Act adds no general transfer requirement.

      Private investigators

      Private investigations are regulated by the Private Investigations Act, published in the Belgian Official Gazette on 6 December 2024 and in force from 16 December 2024, which replaced the Private Detectives Act of 1991. External investigators and in-house investigation services require a five-year permit granted by the Minister of the Interior, with a six-month transition for existing practitioners; HR teams handling employee incidents are exempt from the permit but not from the Act's other rules. The Act sets requirements for interviews, reports and notification to criminal authorities, gives interviewees a right to be assisted, and requires employers to adopt a written investigation policy. As of 2026 the Act is under review by the Constitutional Court.

      Corporate & public records

      The Crossroads Bank for Enterprises (KBO/BCE), operated by the FPS Economy, publishes company name, number, address, legal form, status, activity codes and directors free of charge. It does not list shareholders. Constitutional documents and amendments are published in the annexes to the Belgian Official Gazette, and annual accounts are filed with the Central Balance Sheet Office of the National Bank of Belgium. Registered-share registers are kept by the companies themselves. The UBO register, held by the FPS Finance Treasury, has been restricted since the Royal Decree of 8 February 2023 to authorities, obliged entities and persons demonstrating a legitimate interest.

      Anti-corruption & AML

      Public bribery, including bribery of foreign public officials, is criminalised by Articles 246 to 252 of the Criminal Code and private bribery by Articles 504bis and 504ter, with criminal liability for legal entities. A new Criminal Code is scheduled to take effect on 8 April 2026 and restructures penalties. Anti-money-laundering obligations are set by the Law of 18 September 2017. Enforcement involves the public prosecutor's offices, the Central Office for the Repression of Corruption (OCRC), the financial intelligence unit CTIF-CFI and, for EU budget fraud, the European Public Prosecutor's Office.

      Typical Matters

      Where Investigative Work Involving Belgium Concentrates

      01

      Holding and treasury entity due diligence

      Many groups route European ownership or financing through Belgian entities. We reconcile Crossroads Bank data, Official Gazette publications and National Bank filings to establish who directs a company, how its capital has changed and where the structure leads beyond Belgium, without relying on restricted UBO access.

      02

      Port and logistics fraud

      Antwerp-Bruges handles large volumes of containerised and bulk cargo. Matters include diverted shipments, misdeclared goods, freight-forwarder insolvencies and counterparties that appear and disappear around a single transaction. We map corporate links and public court and insolvency information to show how a scheme was organised.

      03

      EU affairs and procurement integrity

      Organisations dealing with EU institutions, agencies or Belgian public buyers commission reviews of intermediaries, consultants and lobbying relationships. Work focuses on public registers, procurement publications and documented conflicts of interest, framed against Belgian bribery provisions and EU transparency rules.

      04

      Internal investigations in Belgian subsidiaries

      Allegations against staff of a Belgian subsidiary now engage the Private Investigations Act, labour law and the GDPR at once. We help US and international parents scope the work so that interviews, reporting and data handling are run by appropriately permitted investigators or counsel.

      05

      Asset tracing for enforcement

      Where a judgment or award must be enforced against a party with Belgian connections, we identify company interests, directorships and publicly documented property through lawful sources, and support local counsel in deciding where attachment or recognition proceedings are worth pursuing.

      Engagement Standard

      How TDSC Scopes Matters Touching Belgium

      TDSC scopes Belgian matters from Nashville and begins by asking whether the work falls within the Private Investigations Act. Where it does, for example interviews of employees or observation of individuals, the activity is carried out by investigators holding a current Ministry of the Interior permit or handled through Belgian counsel, and TDSC's role is to define questions, coordinate and integrate findings. Research draws on the Crossroads Bank for Enterprises, Official Gazette annexes, National Bank filings, court publications and open sources; TDSC does not seek UBO register access it is not entitled to. Personal data is processed under a documented legitimate-interest assessment, kept to what the question requires, and transferred to the United States only under a GDPR-compliant mechanism agreed with the client.

      TDSC has no office in Belgium. Engagements are directed from Nashville, Tennessee; where work on the ground is needed, it is performed by appropriately licensed local professionals under a written scope. TDSC does not provide legal advice.

      Questions

      Belgium: Frequently Asked Questions

      Does the 2024 Private Investigations Act affect a US company investigating staff in Belgium?

      Yes, potentially. The Act covers internal investigations into employees as well as external investigations, requires permits for investigators other than HR teams handling employee incidents, gives interviewees a right to be assisted and requires a written investigation policy. Non-compliance can lead to fines and to evidence being excluded. Belgian employment counsel should confirm how it applies to a specific matter.

      Can shareholders of a Belgian company be identified from public records?

      Only partly. The Crossroads Bank for Enterprises lists directors but not shareholders, and registered-share registers are kept by the company. Founding shareholders often appear in notarial deeds published in the Official Gazette annexes, and capital changes leave a trail there and in National Bank filings. The UBO register is open only to those with a recognised legitimate interest.

      Which language will Belgian court documents be in?

      It depends on where the proceedings are. Courts in Flanders work in Dutch, those in Wallonia in French, the German-speaking community's court in German, and Brussels courts in Dutch or French. Engagements touching litigation usually need translation planning from the start, and certified translation if documents are to be produced in another jurisdiction.

      Are Belgian company annual accounts available?

      Yes. Most companies must file annual accounts with the Central Balance Sheet Office of the National Bank of Belgium, and these can be consulted and downloaded. Small entities file abridged formats, so the level of detail varies. Late or missing filings are themselves a useful indicator when assessing a counterparty's condition or governance.

      Can TDSC conduct surveillance in Belgium?

      TDSC does not conduct surveillance itself in Belgium. Observation of individuals is an activity regulated by the Private Investigations Act and the GDPR, and it is only considered where it is proportionate and lawful. If a matter genuinely requires it, TDSC coordinates with a permitted Belgian investigator and the client's counsel, who decide whether and how it can proceed.
