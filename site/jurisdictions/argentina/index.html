      Argentine Republic

      Investigations Touching Argentina

      Matters touching Argentina involve a federal civil law system, company registries split between the Inspección General de Justicia and provincial authorities, and one of Latin America's oldest data protection laws. Investigative consulting here means lawful records research, careful scoping of fieldwork and close coordination with Argentine counsel.

      Overview

      Argentina at a Glance

      Argentina has about 45.7 million people (World Bank, 2024) and a federal civil law system. The Civil and Commercial Code governs most private law, and the provinces run their own courts and company registries alongside national institutions. Companies domiciled in the City of Buenos Aires register with the Inspección General de Justicia (IGJ), while those elsewhere register with provincial authorities. Personal data has been protected since 2000 by Law 25,326, known as the habeas data law, and in 2022 Argentina ratified the modernised Council of Europe Convention 108+. Corporate criminal liability for corruption offences has applied since Law 27,401 was published in December 2017.

      Organisations commission work involving Argentina because of its weight in agriculture and food exports, energy (including the Vaca Muerta shale formation), mining of lithium, copper and gold, and a sizeable services and technology sector. Typical exposures include due diligence on local partners and acquisition targets, integrity reviews of counterparties dealing with national, provincial or municipal authorities, asset tracing in support of judgments, arbitral awards and insolvency proceedings, and fraud or diversion by distributors and managers. Periods of currency controls and economic volatility have historically created incentives for asset concealment and informal structures, which careful records research needs to account for.

      Jurisdictional Framework

      The rules that govern lawful inquiry in Argentina

      Legal system

      Argentina has a civil law system under the 1853 Constitution, as amended in 1994. The Civil and Commercial Code, in force since 2015, governs contracts and obligations, and the General Companies Law governs commercial companies. Each province has its own judiciary, and federal courts hear matters within federal jurisdiction. In the City of Buenos Aires, national commercial courts and the National Commercial Court of Appeals hear most corporate and insolvency disputes. The Supreme Court of Justice of the Nation sits at the apex. Commercial arbitration is used in larger contracts, and international commercial arbitration has its own statute.

      Data protection

      Personal Data Protection Law (Law 25,326, 2000) and Regulatory Decree 1558/2001 · Regulator: Agency for Access to Public Information (AAIP)

      Law 25,326 generally requires informed consent for processing, subject to exceptions such as data from public sources or data needed for a contractual relationship. Databases that hold information on individuals must be registered with the National Registry of Databases kept by the AAIP. Transfers to countries without adequate protection are prohibited unless an exception applies, such as express consent. Argentina has an EU adequacy finding and ratified Convention 108+ through Law 27,699 in 2022. The AAIP has promoted reform of the law, but as of 2026 we have not confirmed enactment of a replacement. Investigative reports containing personal data should be scoped with these rules in mind.

      Private investigators

      We have not identified, from sources we could verify, a national licensing regime specific to private investigators in Argentina. Security and investigation services are generally regulated at provincial level, and some provincial and City of Buenos Aires private security laws are understood to address investigative services; we have not confirmed their current terms. In every case, Law 25,326 governs the collection and processing of personal data, the Criminal Code penalises violation of correspondence and unauthorised access to data, and bank and tax records are protected by secrecy rules. Fieldwork, interviews and surveillance are therefore scoped jurisdiction by jurisdiction with Argentine counsel and carried out by appropriately authorised local professionals.

      Corporate & public records

      Companies domiciled in the City of Buenos Aires register with the IGJ, which offers online access to some filings through its open documentation system; companies in the provinces register with provincial registries. The national Ministry of Justice runs a Registro Nacional de Sociedades lookup by tax identifier (CUIT) or company name, drawing on data from the tax agency ARCA, and the underlying dataset is published as open data. We have not confirmed whether beneficial ownership information is publicly accessible. Court records and real estate registries are provincial, and access and searchability vary by jurisdiction.

      Anti-corruption & AML

      Law 27,401, published in December 2017, establishes criminal liability for private legal entities, domestic or foreign, for corruption offences and amends the Criminal Code. The Anti-Corruption Office (Oficina Anticorrupción) promotes integrity programmes, including the voluntary RITE platform for companies, and publishes officials' asset declarations. Money laundering is addressed by Law 25,246 (2000), which created the Financial Information Unit (UIF), the national financial intelligence unit. Federal prosecutors and courts handle criminal enforcement.

      Engagement Standard

      How TDSC Scopes Matters Touching Argentina

      TDSC scopes Argentine matters from Nashville, beginning with a review of where the relevant entities are registered and litigate, since company records, courts and property registries differ between the City of Buenos Aires and each province. We confirm the purpose of any processing of personal data under Law 25,326 and plan how results will be transferred and stored, given its restrictions on transfers to countries without adequate protection. Records research draws on the IGJ, provincial registries, the national company lookup and court systems. Where fieldwork, interviews or document retrieval are needed, we coordinate appropriately authorised local professionals under the rules of the relevant province and work alongside Argentine counsel on evidence and strategy. We do not seek bank or tax records protected by secrecy.

      TDSC has no office in Argentina. Engagements are directed from Nashville, Tennessee; where work on the ground is needed, it is performed by appropriately licensed local professionals under a written scope. TDSC does not provide legal advice.

      Questions

      Argentina: Frequently Asked Questions

      Where are Argentine companies registered?

      It depends on the company's domicile. Companies domiciled in the City of Buenos Aires register with the Inspección General de Justicia, while those in the provinces register with provincial authorities. The national Ministry of Justice also offers a Registro Nacional de Sociedades lookup by CUIT or name, which is a useful starting point before requesting filings from the competent registry.

      Is private investigation licensed in Argentina?

      We have not identified a single national licensing regime for private investigators. Regulation of security and investigation services is largely provincial, and requirements differ between the City of Buenos Aires and the provinces. TDSC therefore confirms the applicable rules with Argentine counsel for each matter and works only with local professionals authorised where the work takes place.

      Can personal data gathered in Argentina be sent to the United States?

      Law 25,326 prohibits transfers to countries without adequate protection unless an exception applies, such as the data subject's express consent. The United States has not been recognised as adequate. Engagements involving Argentine personal data are therefore scoped with counsel to identify a lawful transfer route or to limit what leaves the country, consistent with AAIP guidance.

      Why does Law 27,401 matter for foreign companies?

      Law 27,401 makes legal entities, including foreign companies, criminally liable for corruption offences committed in their name, interest or benefit. Having an adequate integrity programme can be relevant to the consequences. Buyers and partners therefore look for prior investigations, sanctions and public contracts, and assess the integrity arrangements of the counterparty before closing.

      Are Argentine court records publicly searchable?

      Partly. Argentina's courts are organised by province, with separate national and federal courts, and each system has its own case management and access rules. Some systems allow searches of case lists by party name, while files in family and certain criminal matters are restricted. TDSC maps the relevant courts first and works with local counsel where file access requires standing.
