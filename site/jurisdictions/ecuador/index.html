      Republic of Ecuador

      Investigations Touching Ecuador

      Matters touching Ecuador usually involve oil, mining, shrimp, bananas and other exports, a dollarised financial system and a difficult security environment. Investigative consulting here means working within a civil law system, a modern personal data protection law that now has an active regulator, and a company regulator that publishes extensive corporate information.

      Overview

      Ecuador at a Glance

      Ecuador has about 18.3 million people and has used the US dollar as its currency since 2000. It has a civil law system. Companies are supervised by the Superintendence of Companies, Securities and Insurance (Supercias), and the simplified joint-stock company (SAS) has become the most common new company form since 2020. The Organic Law on Personal Data Protection has been in force since May 2021, and its supervisory authority began operating in 2024. Investor reports describe weak judicial independence and uneven contract enforcement. In April 2024 voters rejected a referendum question on recognising international arbitration for investment disputes, which has kept dispute planning a live issue.

      Organisations commission work involving Ecuador because of its export economy. Oil, shrimp (Ecuador became the largest exporter in 2020), bananas, cocoa and cut flowers dominate trade, and mining and critical minerals are attracting new investment. Typical exposures include supply chain integrity and links to illicit activity, the security of transport routes through Guayaquil, illegal mining that encroaches on legal concessions, public procurement and the conduct of state-linked entities, and fraud or asset concealment by counterparties. Since January 2024 the government has treated organised crime as an internal armed conflict, which makes careful counterparty and reputational research more important.

      Typical Matters

      Where Investigative Work Involving Ecuador Concentrates

      01

      Export supply chain integrity

      Ownership, reputation and links to illicit activity for exporters, packers, logistics firms and port service providers in the shrimp, banana, cocoa and flower trades. Buyers increasingly need this research after cocaine was found concealed in legitimate export cargo leaving Guayaquil and other ports.

      02

      Mining concession counterparties

      Background research on concession holders, joint venture partners and local contractors in the mining and critical minerals sector. This includes checking for exposure to illegal mining and community conflict in the affected provinces, and for disputed title over concessions.

      03

      Energy and state-linked contracts

      Integrity due diligence on partners in oil, electricity and infrastructure contracts that involve state entities or concessions in sectors the Constitution reserves to the state. The work focuses on politically exposed persons, procurement history and prior Comptroller or Fiscalía findings.

      04

      Asset tracing and enforcement support

      Identifying companies, shareholdings, real estate and other assets of debtors using Supercias filings, canton-level registries and court records. Findings support Ecuadorian counsel in enforcement, and in assessing recovery prospects where judgments or awards face long delays.

      05

      Executive and partner vetting

      Background checks on prospective executives, distributors and agents. These verify employment, qualifications and litigation history, and they look for exposure to extortion or organised crime, an important consideration while security conditions remain severe in several provinces.

      Jurisdictional Framework

      The rules that govern lawful inquiry in Ecuador

      Legal system

      Ecuador has a civil law system under the 2008 Constitution. Its main codes include the Civil Code, the Commercial Code (updated in 2019) and the Organic Integral Penal Code. The ordinary courts run from first-instance courts to provincial courts of justice and the National Court of Justice. A Constitutional Court rules on constitutional matters, and the Judicial Council administers the judiciary. A specialised court for corruption and organised crime began operating in December 2022. Commercial arbitration is widely used in domestic contracts. Arbitration of investor-state disputes was constrained by the 2024 referendum result.

      Data protection

      Organic Law on Personal Data Protection (LOPDP, 2021) and its General Regulation (2023) · Regulator: Superintendence of Personal Data Protection

      The LOPDP has been in force since May 2021, its Regulation was issued in November 2023, and the Superintendence has been operating since 2024. Lawful bases include consent, legal obligation, court order, contract and legitimate interests that do not override the data subject's rights. Silence does not count as consent. Processing sensitive data is prohibited unless an exception applies. Controllers must register their databases with a national registry, and some must appoint a data protection officer. International transfers require an adequacy finding or safeguards such as contractual clauses, unless an exception applies. Fines can reach 1 percent of turnover.

      Private investigators

      We have not identified, from sources we could verify, a dedicated licensing regime for private investigators in Ecuador. Private security companies operate under separate legislation, and we have not confirmed whether it covers investigative services. Restrictions still apply. The Organic Integral Penal Code criminalises unlawful interception and violations of privacy. The LOPDP governs the collection and processing of personal data. Records held by the tax, social security and financial authorities are not accessible for private inquiries. Any local fieldwork, interviews or surveillance should be scoped with Ecuadorian counsel and carried out by appropriately qualified professionals, with particular attention to physical security.

      Corporate & public records

      Supercias runs an online information portal for companies under its supervision. It typically shows legal status, company documents, administrators, shareholder information and annual financial statements, and compliance certificates indicate whether a company is up to date with its filings. Companies must also register with the tax authority (SRI). Commercial registries kept at canton level record company acts and appointments, and property registries record land. We have not confirmed from a primary source how beneficial ownership is reported to the SRI or whether it is publicly accessible. Court case information is available through the judiciary's online case consultation system.

      Anti-corruption & AML

      Bribery is a criminal offence. Reforms in December 2020 added private-sector corruption, circumvention of procurement procedures and obstruction of justice as offences. An Asset Forfeiture Law was adopted in 2021. Corruption is prosecuted by the Attorney General's Office (Fiscalía General del Estado). The Comptroller General audits public funds, and the Citizen Participation and Social Control Council receives complaints. The Financial and Economic Analysis Unit (UAFE) is the financial intelligence unit. Ecuador ratified UNCAC in 2005 and is not a party to the OECD Anti-Bribery Convention.

      Engagement Standard

      How TDSC Scopes Matters Touching Ecuador

      TDSC handles Ecuador matters from Nashville. We start each one with a jurisdictional review that sets out the lawful basis under the LOPDP for any personal data involved, the records available through Supercias, the canton registries and the courts, and what can only be obtained through counsel or a court order. Most research uses published corporate filings, judicial records, procurement and Comptroller publications, and media. Where fieldwork or record retrieval is needed, we coordinate qualified local professionals and the client's Ecuadorian counsel, and we review security conditions before anyone travels. We keep personal data to what the stated purpose requires and document the transfer safeguards for data sent to the United States.

      TDSC has no office in Ecuador. Engagements are directed from Nashville, Tennessee; where work on the ground is needed, it is performed by appropriately licensed local professionals under a written scope. TDSC does not provide legal advice.

      Questions

      Ecuador: Frequently Asked Questions

      What can be learned from Supercias records?

      Supercias publishes information on the companies it supervises, including legal status, administrators, shareholder information and annual financial statements, as well as certificates showing whether a company has met its filing obligations. Records for SAS companies and older company forms vary in depth. We check filings against canton commercial registry entries and court records.

      Does the LOPDP limit background investigations?

      It does. Each processing activity needs a lawful basis, such as consent, a legal obligation or a legitimate interest that does not override the data subject's rights. Sensitive data is largely off-limits. Since 2024 the Superintendence of Personal Data Protection has been supervising compliance, so we document the purpose and legal basis, minimise what we collect, and apply transfer safeguards when data leaves Ecuador.

      How does the security situation affect investigative work?

      Since January 2024 Ecuador has treated organised crime as an internal armed conflict, and parts of the coast and the mining provinces carry elevated risk. We plan fieldwork with that in mind. Where possible we favour remote and documentary research, and we limit visible inquiries that could endanger sources, staff or the client's own personnel.

      Can international arbitration be relied on in Ecuador?

      That is a question for counsel. Ecuador rejoined ICSID in 2021, but in April 2024 voters rejected recognising international arbitration for investment disputes, and some state-related arbitrations need prior approval. Commercial arbitration between private parties remains common. Our role is to give counsel facts about counterparties and assets, not legal opinions.

      How are corruption risks typically assessed for Ecuadorian partners?

      We look at the partner's ownership and connections to politically exposed persons, its procurement history, any Comptroller or Fiscalía actions, cases in the specialised anti-corruption court, and media reporting. Since the December 2020 reforms made private-sector corruption a crime, a partner's own compliance programme is also relevant to the assessment.
